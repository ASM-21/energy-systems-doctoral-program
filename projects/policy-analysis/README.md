# Policy Analysis and Infrastructure Projects

These projects connect public utility/regulatory evidence to operational-process models and reproducible policy analysis. Keep institutional authority, measured outcomes, and modeled scenarios distinct.

## Current projects

- [Pennsylvania distribution interconnection capstone](pa-distribution-interconnection-capstone/README.md) — descriptive analysis of published Pennsylvania EDC interconnection outcomes, with reconciliation and inference limits.
- [Interconnection queueing and process capacity](interconnection-queueing/README.md) — M/M/c and M/G/1 queueing metrics, Little's Law, staffing sensitivity, and a fixed-seed simulation. Inputs are synthetic.
- [Data requirements](interconnection-queueing/data-requirements.md) — evidence needed before moving from a queueing screen to an empirical process model.
- [PJM/Pennsylvania DER research proposal](pjm-distribution-der-research-proposal.md) — a broader research design proposal, not a completed finding.

## Recommended sequence

Read the Pennsylvania casebook and source register first. Then complete the descriptive capstone and its data-quality audit. Use the queueing model only as a conceptual bridge to service capacity and waiting-time mechanisms. Do not calibrate it to public annual request totals without defensible arrival/service observations and a well-defined process boundary.

## Evidence and confidentiality

Use public primary sources and public aggregate data. Distinguish tariff requirements, Commission orders, proposed rules, stakeholder presentations, utility claims, and independently measured outcomes. Never use confidential employer procedures, customer records, or internal system data. Aggregate annual counts cannot establish individual application duration, approval probability, feeder hosting capacity, or causal policy effects.
