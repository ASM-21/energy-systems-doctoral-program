# DER Orchestration Plan and Behind-the-Meter Storage Review: Regulatory Follow-Through

**Research cut-off:** November 20, 2026  
**Primary dockets:** P-2024-3049223 and R-2025-3057164  
**Scope:** Publicly documented commitments, tariff changes, and public-record status. This is not a representation of non-public PPL project information.

## 1. Executive findings

Two separate follow-through obligations are relevant to PPL's DER management and storage interconnection framework.

1. The PUC's December 3, 2025 order in P-2024-3049223 approved PPL's Second DER Management Plan with modifications. Public PUC testimony later describes the plan as projecting up to 258 MW of additional hosting capacity and notes the Commission directed an RFP for third-party aggregators. The 258 MW is a projected plan benefit, not independently measured feeder headroom.
2. The March 13, 2026 non-unanimous rate-case settlement in R-2025-3057164 contains a commitment for one stakeholder working group before PPL files its DER Orchestration Plan, to the extent the P-2024-3049223 order requires such a plan. The operative settlement body paragraph is **119**. It limits the required working group to parties to the rate proceeding; it is not expressly an open public workshop series.
3. The same settlement requires PPL to review, within 120 days of the final order, BTM non-exporting battery projects then under interconnection review, to assess whether the imposed requirements align with industry best practices and available studies. The operative settlement body paragraph is **124**. With the PUC order entered June 11, 2026, the 120-day period ended October 9, 2026.
4. PPL's publicly linked Energy Storage Interconnection Policy remains Revision 0, effective February 1, 2023. It describes three permitted storage configurations and requires non-export operation. PPL's January 3, 2026 Rule 12 Supplement 411 subsequently includes storage resources (batteries) within the DER interconnection rule and requires smart inverters and utility DER management devices for new DER installations. The two documents need to be read together; the older storage policy's public revision status warrants follow-up.
5. As of this research cut-off, no separately published DER Orchestration Plan, stakeholder working-group materials, or public report describing the results of the 120-day BTM battery review was located in the indexed PPL/PUC public sources searched. This is a **public-record search result**, not proof that no filing or internal review exists. The PUC's Document Search should be checked directly by docket number and document date.

## 3. DER Orchestration Plan stakeholder process

The rate-case settlement body paragraph 119 states that, to the extent PPL must develop a DER Orchestration Plan under P-2024-3049223, PPL agrees to hold one stakeholder working group with the parties to the rate proceeding before filing the plan. At that meeting PPL is to:
- provide substantive updates on the process and anticipated plan content;
- address stakeholder questions;
- solicit stakeholder feedback; and
- consider the feedback in good faith when finalizing the plan.

**Interpretation:** This is a procedural engagement commitment, not a requirement for a multi-month public technical collaborative. The wording identifies the parties to R-2025-3057164 as the working-group participants. Do not imply that every DER developer or member of the public has an automatic right to attend.

**Numbering discrepancy:** In the settlement's operative body, the DER Orchestration Plan commitment is paragraph 119. Appendix D's proposed findings 388–390 cite it as Settlement paragraph 118. Paragraph 118 in the settlement body is the IT-upgrade provision. Use the operative body paragraph number and record the Appendix D cross-reference error.

The original P-2024-3049223 order is the controlling source for the plan's substantive scope and filing deadline. The March 2026 rate-case settlement adds the one-working-group commitment but does not restate the full technical deliverable. The plan itself was not located in the indexed public sources searched as of November 20, 2026. Verify through the PUC Document Search by docket before concluding that it has not been filed.

## 4. BTM non-exporting battery review

The settlement's operative body paragraph 124 states that PPL will review, within 120 days of entry of the final order, BTM non-exporting battery storage projects then being reviewed for interconnection behind a customer's meter. The purpose is to determine whether the requirements being imposed align with industry best practices and available studies.

The PUC entered its order on June 11, 2026, making October 9, 2026 the 120-day date. The commitment:
- applies to projects already under review, not necessarily all future battery applications;
- requires a review of the requirements being imposed;
- does not itself prescribe the review methodology or a specific technical standard;
- does not expressly require a public report, tariff filing, or revised policy by the deadline.

**Numbering discrepancy:** Appendix D's proposed finding 443 refers to Settlement paragraph 123, while the operative settlement body labels the BTM review paragraph 124. Paragraph 123 in the settlement body concerns supplier-tariff changes. Use paragraph 124.

No separate public report documenting the outcome of this review was located in the indexed sources searched. The obligation may have been completed internally without a public deliverable. A direct docket search is necessary before concluding otherwise.

## 5. Technical policy crosswalk

### PPL Energy Storage Interconnection Policy, Revision 0

The publicly linked policy (EU00565314) is dated February 1, 2023. It describes three configurations:

1. Standby/backup storage under NEC 702, with open-transition switching and discharge only when electrically isolated from the grid.
2. Storage operating in parallel with the utility without on-site generation, as a demand-side-management resource, but without export.
3. Storage operating in parallel with on-site generation, also without storage export.

The policy says that parallel ESS applications require an interconnection review and signed interconnection agreement; at the time of publication, storage nameplate was not considered in the interconnection review because storage was not net-metering eligible and export was prohibited. It requires verifiable proof of non-export controls and reserves the right to inspect/verify non-export status. It excludes storage interconnections operating under a PJM Wholesale Market Participation Agreement.

### Rule 12, Supplement 411

PPL issued Supplement 411 on January 2, 2026, effective January 3, 2026. It expressly includes storage resources (batteries) in the DER definition. For new DER interconnection applications received on or after January 3, 2026, Rule 12 requires:
- IEEE 1547-2018-compliant smart inverters certified to UL 1741 Supplement B or current utility certification requirements;
- a PPL-owned, installed, and maintained DER Management Device connected to the smart inverter data port, subject to the tariff's compatibility exception; and
- PPL's ability to actively monitor and manage inverter grid-support functions through DERMS.

The current PPL public Point-of-Contact Requirements page still lists the Energy Storage Policy Revision 0, dated 2023. The existence of the later Rule 12 supplement does not by itself establish that the storage policy has been superseded in full. It does establish that the public document set should be reconciled and that a reviewer should not rely on the 2023 guidance alone for a new 2026 application.

## 6. Questions for the public-record review

### DER Orchestration Plan
- Has PPL filed the plan in P-2024-3049223?
- What is the controlling order's deadline and required scope?
- Was the R-2025-3057164 stakeholder working group held? What parties attended?
- Were agendas, presentations, comments, or meeting summaries filed?

- Does it include technical criteria, telemetry, customer consent, cybersecurity, curtailment priority, compensation, and upgrade-cost treatment?

### BTM battery review
- What population of applications was in review on June 11, 2026?
- Did the review distinguish non-exporting storage-only, solar-plus-storage, and storage under a PJM WMPA?
- Did it examine inadvertent export, reverse power, protection coordination, IEEE 1547 ride-through, export limiting, metering, and telemetry?
- What industry standards and studies were considered?
- Did PPL change its application screens, technical requirements, tariff, or policy after the review?
- Was any public report filed, or was the review treated as an internal compliance task?

## 7. Research implications

This record supports a useful regulatory implementation study: compare the 2023 storage guidance, 2026 Rule 12 tariff requirements, the 2025 DER Management Plan order, and the 2026 settlement commitments. The central engineering-policy question is whether non-exporting BTM batteries are assessed using requirements proportional to their actual operating envelope and risk, while retaining protection against inadvertent export and unsafe parallel operation.

Do not infer that a battery is harmless merely because it is configured not to export. Charging can increase feeder loading; discharging can reduce net load; control failure can create inadvertent export; and protection/voltage effects depend on inverter settings and feeder conditions. Equally, do not infer that every battery requires the same monitoring/control hardware without examining the system size, inverter functionality, export controls, network location, and available alternatives.

The IEEE 13-node OpenDSS benchmark in this repository can support method demonstrations, but it is not a substitute for PPL feeder data or the public regulatory record. Any empirical estimate of PPL-specific BTM battery effects would require project-level and feeder-level data not present in EIA-861.
