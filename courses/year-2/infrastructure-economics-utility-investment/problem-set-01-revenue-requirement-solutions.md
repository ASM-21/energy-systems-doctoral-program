# Solution Notes: Revenue Requirement, Rate Base, and WACC

These notes provide benchmark calculations. They do not replace a learner's explanation of assumptions, institutional context, and limitations.

## Problem 1

Allowed return:

`0.076 × $900m = $68.4m`

Simplified revenue requirement:

`180 + 42 + 8 + 68.4 - 5 = $293.4m/year`

This is a revenue requirement, not a volumetric tariff. A tariff allocates approved costs across customer classes and billing determinants. Actual rate design may include customer charges, kWh charges, demand charges, time-of-use periods, riders, taxes, and other components.

Omitted items may include working capital, accumulated deferred income taxes, regulatory assets/liabilities, income-tax normalization, pension expense, rate-case expense, cost allocation, jurisdictional adjustments, fuel/purchased-power mechanisms, and other revenue credits. The appropriate list depends on the utility and proceeding.

## Problem 2

After-tax WACC:

`0.45 × 5.2% × (1 - 0.25) + 0.55 × 10.0% = 7.255%`

Ignoring the debt tax shield:

`0.45 × 5.2% + 0.55 × 10.0% = 7.84%`

The regulatory allowed return is determined in a proceeding using an evidentiary record and applicable law. It may be based on a test-year capital structure, cost-of-debt evidence, and a commission determination of the cost of equity. It need not equal the utility's realized accounting return or a market estimate at a later date.

Higher leverage increases fixed financing obligations and can raise equity risk, refinancing risk, and credit-rating pressure. A changed capital structure can also affect tax shields and the weighted cost of capital. In practice, the relationship is not mechanically linear because debt costs and equity costs may themselves change with leverage.

## Problem 3

Annual straight-line depreciation:

`$120m / 30 = $4m/year`

Year 1:
- Beginning net plant: $120m.
- Ending net plant: $116m.
- Average net plant: $118m.
- Return component: `0.076 × $118m = $8.968m`.

Using original cost of $120m every year would ignore accumulated depreciation and therefore overstate the return component as the plant depreciates, under the stated simplified average-rate-base convention.

Construction work in progress may or may not earn a return depending on jurisdictional rules and whether an allowance for funds used during construction is applied. Accumulated deferred income taxes can reduce rate base in some regulatory treatments because customers have effectively provided an interest-free source of capital through tax timing differences. The actual accounting and order must be examined.

## Problem 4

Residential allocation:

`$24m × 0.55 = $13.2m`

Average residential annual increase:

`$13.2m / 600,000 = $22.00/account/year`

Average residential monthly increase:

`$22 / 12 = $1.8333/account/month`

Commercial allocation:

`$24m × 0.45 = $10.8m`

Average commercial annual increase:

`$10.8m / 100,000 = $108/account/year`

Average commercial monthly increase:

`$108 / 12 = $9/account/month`

These are class averages under equal within-class allocation. Actual bills depend on usage, demand, customer class, tariff design, season, load shape, and any customer-specific riders or credits. Volumetric modeling needs kWh by billing period; demand charges need the applicable kW determinant and measurement interval; time-of-use rates need interval consumption mapped to tariff periods. Customer counts and total class allocations alone are insufficient.

## Problem 5

- **Application:** Establishes what the utility requested and the arguments/data it submitted. It is not proof that the requested treatment was approved.
- **Utility testimony:** Establishes a witness's sworn position and supporting analysis, subject to discovery and cross-examination. It is not a commission finding.
- **Intervenor testimony:** Establishes an opposing or alternative evidentiary position, also subject to testing. It is not automatically accepted.
- **Settlement petition:** Establishes the parties' negotiated proposal and requested disposition. A settlement is not operative merely because parties filed it.
- **Commission order:** Establishes the regulator's approved disposition, including modifications, conditions, findings, and effective-date instructions. The order's exact language matters.
- **Tariff filing:** Shows the tariff sheets submitted or accepted for implementation. Check commission approval/effectiveness and the current tariff record before claiming a rate is in force.

The commission order controls the approved outcome. The tariff sheets and their approval/effective status establish the rates customers are billed under. A settlement appendix may contain proposed findings drafted by parties; the order may adopt, reject, modify, or leave those statements unaddressed. Cite the actual order before attributing a factual finding to the commission.

## Problem 6

From Problem 1, the baseline revenue requirement is $293.4m.

1. Rate base +$100m: incremental return `0.076 × $100m = $7.6m`. New requirement: $301.0m.
2. Allowed return +0.50 percentage points: incremental return `0.005 × $900m = $4.5m`. New requirement: $297.9m.
3. OPEX +3%: incremental expense `0.03 × $180m = $5.4m`. New requirement: $298.8m.

These are static sensitivities, not forecasts of customer bills or utility earnings. Between rate cases, shareholders may bear some cost variance under conventional regulation, but riders, trackers, formula rates, deferrals, and later prudence review can shift some costs or timing to customers. Construction-cost overruns may be disallowed if imprudent; prudent capital additions may enter rate base only under the applicable rules. A change in the allowed return is a regulatory decision, not an automatic pass-through of market rates.

## Grading emphasis

Full credit requires correct units and calculations, a distinction between allowed and realized returns, careful document-status analysis, and an explicit account of who bears risk and when. Treating the simplified identity as a complete real-world tariff model is a substantive error.
