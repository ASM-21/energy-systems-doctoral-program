# Problem Set 1: Revenue Requirement, Rate Base, and WACC

**Level:** Graduate foundations  
**Suggested effort:** 3–4 hours  
**Prerequisites:** Introductory finance, algebra, and Y1-03 utility regulation concepts

Show equations, units, intermediate steps, and assumptions. Unless otherwise stated, treat all dollar amounts as nominal dollars for one test year. These are stylized teaching cases, not estimates for a named utility.

## Problem 1 — Construct a simplified revenue requirement

A regulated distribution utility has the following proposed test-year values:

| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Operating expenses | $180 million |
| Depreciation expense | $42 million |
| Tax expense | $8 million |
| Rate base | $900 million |
| Allowed rate of return | 7.6% |
| Other operating revenues credited to this service | $5 million |

1. Calculate the allowed return on rate base.
2. Calculate the simplified annual revenue requirement.
3. Explain why the result is not a retail rate in dollars per kWh.
4. Identify at least four items that a real rate case may include but this simplified model omits.

## Problem 2 — Capital structure and WACC

Assume a regulatory capital structure of 45% debt and 55% equity. The pre-tax cost of debt is 5.2%, the cost of equity is 10.0%, and the applicable tax rate for this simplified exercise is 25%.

1. Calculate the after-tax WACC using the conventional tax-shield expression.
2. Calculate the WACC if the tax shield is ignored.
3. Explain why the regulatory allowed return may differ from a utility's contemporaneous accounting return or market-implied cost of capital.
4. State two reasons why a change in capital structure can alter financial risk even if the total capital requirement is unchanged.

## Problem 3 — Rate base roll-forward

A project enters service at the beginning of Year 1 with original cost of $120 million. Assume straight-line depreciation over 30 years, no salvage value, no additions or retirements, and a simplified average rate base equal to the average of beginning- and end-of-year net plant.

1. Calculate annual depreciation.
2. Calculate beginning, ending, and average net plant for Year 1.
3. Calculate the Year 1 return component at an allowed return of 7.6%.
4. Explain why using original cost as the rate base every year would overstate the return component under these assumptions.
5. Explain how construction work in progress and accumulated deferred income taxes could complicate this simplified calculation.

## Problem 4 — Revenue requirement versus customer bill impact

Suppose a rate case increases the annual revenue requirement by $24 million. The utility serves 600,000 residential accounts and 100,000 commercial accounts. Residential accounts contribute 55% of the allocated increase; commercial accounts contribute 45%.

1. Calculate the average annual increase per residential account and per commercial account, assuming equal allocation within each class.
2. Convert each to an average monthly amount.
3. Explain why these averages do not predict an individual customer's bill impact.
4. Identify the additional billing determinants needed to model volumetric, demand, and time-of-use tariffs.

## Problem 5 — Evidence and regulatory status

You are reviewing a public rate proceeding. The record contains:
- a utility application;
- direct testimony from the utility's witness;
- an intervenor's testimony;
- a joint settlement petition;
- a commission order approving the settlement with modifications;
- a later tariff filing implementing new rates.

For each document, explain what it can establish and what it cannot establish by itself. Identify which document controls the approved outcome and which document must be checked to confirm the actual tariff in effect. Explain why a proposed finding in a settlement appendix should not automatically be described as an adjudicated commission finding.

## Problem 6 — Sensitivity and risk allocation

Return to Problem 1. Calculate the change in the simplified revenue requirement if:
- rate base increases by $100 million while all else is constant;
- the allowed return increases by 50 basis points while all else is constant;
- operating expenses increase by 3%.

Then identify who may bear each risk under a conventional cost-of-service framework, and explain why the answer depends on the timing of the next rate case, any adjustment mechanism, prudence review, and the governing order.
