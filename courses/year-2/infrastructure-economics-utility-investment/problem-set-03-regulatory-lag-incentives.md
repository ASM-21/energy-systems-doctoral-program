# Problem Set 3: Regulatory Lag, Incentives, and Performance Regulation

**Level:** Graduate applied regulatory economics  
**Suggested effort:** 3–4 hours  
**Prerequisites:** Problem Sets 1–2; introductory microeconomics and finance

This assignment uses a stylized distribution utility. It is designed to isolate incentive mechanisms, not to reproduce a particular state's statute, tariff, or commission practice. State every assumption and distinguish a mechanism's theoretical incentive from its actual implementation.

## Case assumptions

A commission-approved rate plan establishes a test-year operating-cost allowance of $100 million. For the first three plan years, nominal retail rates remain unchanged unless an explicitly described adjustment mechanism applies. Ignore taxes, financing, depreciation, sales changes, and all revenue-requirement components other than the operating-cost allowance for Problems 1–3.

Without a new efficiency initiative, actual prudent operating costs are forecast to be:

| Plan year | Operating cost without initiative |
|---|---:|
| 1 | $104.00m |
| 2 | $108.16m |
| 3 | $112.49m |

A management initiative costs $2 million in Year 1 and reduces operating cost by $5 million in each of Years 1–3. For the simplified arithmetic, treat the $2 million implementation cost as an expense in Year 1. The initiative does not change service quality, reliability, safety, or output in the base case. In practice, that assumption must be tested rather than presumed.

## Problem 1 — Static rates and regulatory lag

1. Under the unchanged-rate plan, calculate the difference between the $100 million allowed operating-cost allowance and actual operating cost in each year without the initiative.
2. Repeat with the initiative, including the $2 million Year 1 implementation expense.
3. Calculate the initiative's incremental annual operating result relative to the no-initiative case in each year.
4. Explain why a negative difference between the allowance and actual cost is not automatically the utility's total accounting loss or the amount of shareholder return. Identify omitted revenue-requirement and financial items.
5. Explain how regulatory lag can create incentives to reduce costs while also exposing the utility to inflation, load, and cost shocks.

## Problem 2 — Annual cost-of-service reset

Now assume, solely for this problem, that the operating-cost allowance is reset each year to equal the actual prudent operating cost for that year, with no lag and no sharing mechanism.

1. Calculate the allowed operating-cost amount in each year without the initiative and with the initiative.
2. Calculate the customer-side reduction in the allowed operating-cost requirement attributable to the initiative in each year.
3. Compare the utility's ability to retain the cost savings under this stylized annual reset with the unchanged-rate case.
4. Explain why an actual rate case does not necessarily reset every cost to actual expenditure, and why prudence, used-and-useful, normalization, forecasting, and evidentiary rules matter.

## Problem 3 — Earnings-sharing mechanism

Suppose a sharing mechanism returns 50% of verified operating-cost savings relative to a defined benchmark to customers, while the utility retains 50%. For this problem, compare the initiative with the no-initiative cost path and assume all $5 million annual savings are verified. Treat the $2 million implementation cost as a Year 1 cost included in the initiative's net savings.

1. Calculate net verified savings in Years 1–3.
2. Calculate the customer share and utility-retained share in each year.
3. Compare the total three-year amounts with the unchanged-rate and annual-reset cases.
4. Explain why the benchmark, sharing threshold, quality adjustments, audit rights, and treatment of one-time costs determine the actual incentive.
5. Discuss the possibility that a sharing mechanism can create incentives to shift costs between operating expense and capital expenditure.

## Problem 4 — Distinguish regulatory mechanisms

For each mechanism below, explain the revenue or cost variable it adjusts, the principal risk it reallocates, and one potential unintended consequence:

- Cost-of-service rate case with rates fixed between cases.
- Formula rate or annual revenue-requirement true-up.
- Fuel or purchased-power adjustment clause.
- Revenue decoupling mechanism.
- Earnings-sharing mechanism.
- Performance-based regulation with output or outcome metrics.
- Multi-year rate plan with an efficiency carryover or retention period.

Do not treat these mechanisms as interchangeable. In particular, revenue decoupling addresses the relationship between sales volume and authorized revenue; it does not, by itself, guarantee recovery of every cost or reward cost efficiency.

## Problem 5 — Output quality and performance incentives

A utility reports a 7% reduction in controllable operating expense after a new performance plan. During the same period, its SAIDI and SAIFI values worsen, customer complaints increase, and planned maintenance is deferred.

1. Explain why the cost reduction alone is insufficient evidence of improved productivity or welfare.
2. Design a balanced performance scorecard with at least five metrics spanning reliability, safety, customer service, affordability, and cost efficiency.
3. Define the denominator and measurement period for each metric. Identify at least two risks of metric gaming.
4. Explain how a regulator might use deadbands, caps, collars, multi-year averages, audit provisions, and service-quality penalties.
5. Discuss the difference between an output metric (e.g., outage duration) and an outcome or welfare measure (e.g., customer interruption cost), including measurement challenges.

## Problem 6 — Regulatory design memo

Prepare a 700–1,000 word memo for a hypothetical state commission considering a three-year distribution rate plan.

Your memo must:
- define the policy problem and regulatory objectives;
- identify which risks should be borne by customers, shareholders, or shared;
- recommend a coherent combination of rate-plan length, cost trackers, revenue decoupling (if justified), and performance metrics;
- explain how the mechanism preserves incentives for efficient operating and capital decisions;
- address service quality, affordability, safety, and cost-shifting risks;
- specify data, audit, and review requirements;
- identify the evidence needed before applying the design to an actual utility.

## Submission and evaluation

Submit a calculation workbook or script, a mechanism comparison table, and the memo. Evaluation: quantitative accuracy (25%), mechanism distinctions (20%), incentive analysis (20%), performance measurement (15%), regulatory reasoning and evidence (10%), reproducibility and limitations (10%).

A strong answer recognizes that no regulatory mechanism eliminates risk. It reallocates risk, changes incentives, and creates monitoring requirements.
