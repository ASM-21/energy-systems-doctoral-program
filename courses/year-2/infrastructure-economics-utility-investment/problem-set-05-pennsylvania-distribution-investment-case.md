# Problem Set 5: Pennsylvania Distribution Investment and DER Integration Case

**Level:** Graduate applied regulatory economics and infrastructure policy  
**Suggested effort:** 5–7 hours  
**Prerequisites:** Problem Sets 1–4; Y1-01 power systems, Y1-03 regulatory institutions, and basic empirical data handling

This is a public-record case study. Do not use employer systems, internal work products, customer records, nonpublic planning data, or confidential information. Select a Pennsylvania electric distribution utility and a public distribution investment or rate proceeding. PPL Electric Utilities may be selected, but the assignment does not assume that any particular utility proposal is prudent or that interconnection volume proves a need for a specific capital project.

## Case context and evidence boundary

The [Pennsylvania DER interconnection casebook](../../../research/casebooks/pa-der-interconnection/README.md) contains a public-data baseline, source register, responsibility matrix, and research limitations. Its 2023–2025 aggregate interconnection dataset documents request volumes and reported process outcomes by electric distribution company. It is useful for describing administrative workload and motivating research questions. It does not, by itself, establish:
- the physical location or magnitude of distribution constraints;
- the cost of specific upgrades or the utility's incremental capital requirement;
- project-level customer benefits or avoided costs;
- the cause of an application's processing time or outcome;
- the prudence, used-and-useful status, or rate recovery of a proposed investment.

Your case must distinguish distribution interconnection, distribution system planning, wholesale DER aggregation, transmission interconnection, and retail rate recovery. Identify the institution with authority over each decision in the selected record.

## Required case selection

Choose one public Pennsylvania electric distribution utility proceeding involving distribution investment, grid modernization, reliability, DER integration, interconnection, or base-rate recovery. You may use a rate case, a targeted program approval, a distribution planning filing, or a commission rulemaking only if the selected record contains enough evidence to analyze investment costs and decision rights.

Record:
1. Utility, docket number, proceeding title, filing date, test year or planning horizon, and relevant service territory.
2. Procedural status at the date of your review: application, testimony, settlement proposal, commission order, tariff filing, or effective tariff.
3. The exact primary documents used, their dates, and their legal/evidentiary status.
4. Whether the proceeding is final, pending, partially approved, or superseded. Do not treat a settlement as an operative order unless the commission adopted it.
5. The scope of investment actually at issue. Separate proposed, approved, in-service, and recovered costs.

## Problem 1 — Institutional and procedural map

Construct a decision-rights map for the selected investment. Identify the roles of the utility, Pennsylvania PUC, PJM, FERC, customers/intervenors, and any relevant local or state agencies. Do not assign authority based only on an organization's participation in a stakeholder process.

Explain which questions are:
- distribution planning or engineering;
- physical interconnection review;
- wholesale market participation;
- transmission planning or interconnection;
- retail cost allocation and rate recovery;
- environmental or siting approval.

Cite the primary authority or proceeding document for each material claim.

## Problem 2 — Investment and revenue-requirement reconstruction

Reconstruct the most defensible available estimate of the investment's cost and recovery treatment.

1. Identify proposed capital cost, annual O&M, project timing, expected service life, depreciation treatment, and any requested return or rider treatment.
2. Separate gross project cost, grant/contribution offsets, customer-funded contributions, capitalized costs, and amounts proposed for rate recovery.
3. Identify whether costs are forecast, actual, audited, approved, or merely illustrative.
4. Where data permit, calculate a simplified annual revenue requirement or incremental revenue requirement. Show rate base, depreciation, return, O&M, and tax assumptions separately.
5. If the public record does not disclose sufficient inputs, do not manufacture an estimate. State what is missing and provide a bounded or symbolic calculation instead.

## Problem 3 — Cost allocation and bill incidence

1. Identify the proposed customer classes and billing determinants that bear the cost.
2. Evaluate whether the allocation method is consistent with the stated cost drivers. Distinguish customer-, demand-, energy-, location-, and reliability-related drivers.
3. Calculate representative bill impacts only if the source record provides enough information. Otherwise, identify the minimum additional data required.
4. Explain whether benefits and costs fall on the same customers, classes, or locations.
5. Test at least one alternative allocation or recovery design and identify who gains and loses.

A class-average bill impact is not a prediction for each customer. Do not infer affordability from a tariff rate alone.

## Problem 4 — Engineering need and alternatives

Identify the engineering need the investment is intended to address. Assess whether the record provides evidence of:
- thermal, voltage, protection, power-quality, or reliability constraints;
- load growth, DER penetration, hosting capacity, or interconnection requests;
- alternatives such as operational changes, non-wires alternatives, flexible interconnection, storage, targeted upgrades, or demand response;
- project scope, design criteria, contingencies, and expected utilization.

Explain what the record establishes and what it does not. A high interconnection request count is not itself proof of a binding feeder constraint or a particular upgrade requirement.

## Problem 5 — Capital budgeting, uncertainty, and risk allocation

Construct a project-risk register covering at least:
- construction cost and schedule;
- load and DER utilization;
- technology and integration;
- operational performance and reliability;
- regulatory approval and cost recovery;
- stranded-asset or policy risk.

For each risk, identify the party best positioned to manage it, who bears it under the proposed mechanism, and whether the allocation creates appropriate incentives.

Where public inputs permit, conduct NPV or scenario analysis. State whose perspective is used: utility, customers, or society. Avoid counting rate payments as societal benefits or counting avoided outage benefits twice. If key data are absent, use a transparent parameterized model and identify the evidence needed to populate it.

## Problem 6 — Regulatory design recommendation

Recommend whether the commission should approve, modify, defer, or reject the selected proposal, or explain why the available record does not support a recommendation. Your recommendation must address:
- demonstrated need and alternatives;
- prudence, scope, procurement, and project governance;
- revenue-requirement and rate-design treatment;
- affordability and distributional effects;
- performance measures and post-investment review;
- data transparency, audit, and reporting;
- uncertainty and the consequences of forecast error.

Do not confuse a policy preference with a finding established by the record. State the strongest counterargument to your recommendation.

## Required deliverables

1. **Investment memo:** 2,000–3,000 words, written for a commission staff or senior policy audience.
2. **Reproducible workbook or script:** source-linked assumptions, calculations, units, sensitivity cases, and reconciliation checks.
3. **Source register:** primary documents, publication dates, docket identifiers, status, relevant pages/sections, and the claim each source supports.
4. **Institutional responsibility map:** one-page table or diagram.
5. **Risk register:** risk, probability/severity rationale, controllability, bearer, mitigation, and evidence gap.
6. **Limitations statement:** explicit distinction between known facts, party assertions, commission findings, model assumptions, and unknowns.

## Evaluation rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Primary-source quality, procedural status, and institutional accuracy | 20% |
| Engineering need, alternatives, and evidence boundaries | 15% |
| Revenue requirement, cost allocation, and bill analysis | 20% |
| Capital budgeting, uncertainty, and risk allocation | 20% |
| Regulatory recommendation and counterargument | 15% |
| Reproducibility, source traceability, and limitations | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

A strong case can conclude that the record is insufficient. Unsupported precision, conflating proposed and approved costs, or inferring physical need from aggregate queue counts will be penalized.

## Research integrity

Use only publicly accessible material. Cite page, paragraph, table, docket, or tariff references precisely. Preserve source versions and access dates. If a document is superseded or amended, explain which version governs the analysis. Never place confidential employer or customer information in the repository.
