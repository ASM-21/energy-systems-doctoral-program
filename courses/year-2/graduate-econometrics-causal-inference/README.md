# Graduate Econometrics and Causal Inference for Energy Policy

**Status:** Planned Year 2 course  
**Duration:** 24–30 modular weeks across two semesters  
**Weekly target:** 2–3 hours, within the program's total 3–5 hour weekly budget  
**Level:** Advanced graduate / doctoral preparation  
**Purpose:** Develop the ability to identify, estimate, validate, and communicate causal effects in energy infrastructure, utility regulation, DER adoption, electricity markets, and public policy.

## Why this course is necessary

Energy-policy datasets are rarely generated by randomized assignment. Utility investment, interconnection rules, customer adoption, and market participation respond to project characteristics, network constraints, economic conditions, and policy choices. A regression coefficient is not automatically a causal effect. This course emphasizes estimands, assignment mechanisms, counterfactual assumptions, and research designs before estimator selection.

The course should support the Pennsylvania DER interconnection capstone, but it is not limited to that case. Other applications include transmission investment, storage incentives, renewable deployment, demand response, rate design, reliability programs, and energy-efficiency policy.

## Prerequisites

- Calculus, linear algebra, probability, and introductory statistics.
- Introductory econometrics and regression, including OLS and robust standard errors.
- Python or R for data cleaning, estimation, visualization, and reproducibility.
- Basic understanding of energy institutions and the policy question being studied.

Complete the Year 1 quantitative methods course and review the [OLS and robust inference lab](../../../projects/econometrics/ols-energy-demand/README.md) before beginning. The [competing-risks survival lab](../../../projects/econometrics/competing-risks-survival/README.md) is a companion for event-history methods.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the learner should be able to:

1. Define potential outcomes, treatment assignment, estimands, and identification assumptions.
2. Distinguish descriptive, predictive, and causal claims.
3. Derive omitted-variable bias and explain selection into treatment.
4. Design and analyze randomized experiments, including intention-to-treat and treatment-on-the-treated estimands.
5. Estimate and diagnose panel fixed-effects and difference-in-differences designs.
6. Evaluate staggered treatment timing, heterogeneous effects, and event-study estimators.
7. Formulate instrumental-variable estimands and assess relevance, exclusion, independence, and monotonicity.
8. Design and audit regression-discontinuity studies.
9. Understand synthetic control, time-series interventions, and panel counterfactual methods.
10. Model time-to-event outcomes, competing risks, and multi-state processes.
11. Address clustering, serial correlation, spillovers, interference, and multiple testing.
12. Produce a reproducible research package with a defensible identification argument and policy interpretation.

## Course sequence

| Module | Topics | Energy-policy application |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Causal foundations | Potential outcomes, counterfactuals, estimands, SUTVA, DAGs, assignment mechanisms | Define effects of interconnection process changes |
| 1. Randomized experiments | Random assignment, ITT, TOT, compliance, randomization inference, precision | Randomized application assistance or pilot-program offers |
| 2. Regression and selection | OLS, conditional independence, omitted-variable bias, functional form, weighting | Project mix and interconnection outcomes |
| 3. Panel data | Fixed effects, first differences, clustered inference, dynamic panels | EDC/feeder-year outcomes and utility investment |
| 4. Difference-in-differences | Parallel trends, event studies, treatment heterogeneity, staggered adoption | Regulatory and DERMS policy changes |
| 5. Instrumental variables | 2SLS, LATE, weak instruments, exclusion, monotonicity, overidentification | Quasi-random policy eligibility or geography-based instruments |
| 6. Regression discontinuity | Sharp/fuzzy designs, bandwidth, continuity, manipulation, local effects | Program size thresholds and tariff eligibility |
| 7. Synthetic control | Donor pools, pre-fit, placebo tests, inference, multiple treated units | Utility-specific policy or investment interventions |
| 8. Survival and competing risks | Kaplan-Meier, Aalen-Johansen, cause-specific hazards, multi-state models | Application time-to-disposition and PTO |
| 9. Time series and forecasting | Stationarity, unit roots, cointegration, ARIMA, structural breaks, HAC | Load, prices, investment and DER trends |
| 10. Interference and spatial designs | Spillovers, network exposure, partial interference, spatial dependence | Shared feeders, neighboring projects, congestion displacement |
| 11. Research synthesis | Design-based reasoning, robustness, sensitivity, external validity, replication | Integrated energy-policy evaluation |

Use the [pre-analysis plan template](pre-analysis-plan-template.md) to freeze the estimand, treatment definition, identification assumptions, primary outcome, inference method, censoring rules, power assumptions, and robustness checks before inspecting outcomes.

## Assessment

- Problem sets and derivations: 30%.
- Reproducible computational assignments: 25%.
- Research design critique and replication: 20%.
- Final empirical or policy-evaluation proposal: 25%.

A passing assignment must state the estimand and identification assumptions before presenting an estimator. Robustness checks do not rescue a fundamentally unidentified design.

## Connection to the Pennsylvania capstone

The [PA distribution DER interconnection capstone](../../../projects/policy-analysis/pa-distribution-interconnection-capstone/README.md) currently supports descriptive analysis only. Its 2023–2025 aggregate panel predates the 2026 policy changes and lacks application-level outcomes. This course develops the design needed for a future evaluation if suitable application-level data become available. Do not treat the existing panel as a natural experiment.
