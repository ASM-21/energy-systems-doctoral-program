# Year 2 — Advanced Analytical Methods

**Status:** Planned sequence; activate after Year 1 progression gates are met.  
**Workload:** Continue to target 3–5 hours/week total. Do not stack every advanced course concurrently.

## Purpose

Move from competent use of energy models toward independent formulation, mathematical analysis, empirical identification, and computational implementation.

## Planned courses

1. [Advanced Mathematical Methods for Energy Systems and Infrastructure Research](advanced-mathematics-energy-systems/README.md) — planned two-semester sequence covering matrix analysis, calculus and analysis, dynamical systems, probability, convex optimization, numerical methods, graph theory, and stochastic/robust optimization.
2. [Graduate Econometrics and Causal Inference for Energy Policy](graduate-econometrics-causal-inference/README.md) — potential outcomes, randomized designs, panel methods, difference-in-differences, IV, RD, synthetic control, survival analysis, competing risks, time series, and interference. Begin with [Problem Set 1](graduate-econometrics-causal-inference/problem-set-01-potential-outcomes.md) and its [worked solutions](graduate-econometrics-causal-inference/problem-set-01-potential-outcomes-solutions.md).
3. Electricity Market Design and Optimization — OPF, unit commitment, reserve co-optimization, market power, transmission constraints, capacity mechanisms, and equilibrium.
4. Infrastructure Economics and Utility Investment — revenue requirements, cost of capital, rate design, regulatory incentives, planning, and risk allocation.

The advanced mathematics course includes an optional [queueing theory extension](advanced-mathematics-energy-systems/problem-set-10-queueing-theory-interconnection.md) for process-capacity and interconnection-review research. It is not part of the minimum Year 2 workload.

## Recommended sequencing

- First: mathematical prerequisite diagnostic and targeted remediation.
- Next: matrix methods, multivariable calculus, probability, and convex optimization modules.
- In parallel only where manageable: econometrics and electricity market design.
- Later: stochastic optimization, robust planning, and advanced infrastructure models.

## Year 2 evidence

The year should culminate in a reproducible paper or technical study. The [Pennsylvania distribution DER interconnection capstone](../../projects/policy-analysis/pa-distribution-interconnection-capstone/README.md) is a concrete public-data case study connecting network engineering, utility regulation, empirical identification, and stochastic investment design. The year-end study should contain:
- a clearly defined research question and literature positioning;
- explicit mathematical formulation and assumptions;
- an appropriate empirical or computational method;
- sensitivity and uncertainty analysis;
- independent validation or robustness checks;
- a policy or infrastructure interpretation;
- a limitations statement and reproducibility package.

The advanced mathematics course should support the other Year 2 courses, not become a detached exercise in abstract mathematics. Every major mathematical topic should be connected to a model, empirical method, or decision problem in energy systems.
