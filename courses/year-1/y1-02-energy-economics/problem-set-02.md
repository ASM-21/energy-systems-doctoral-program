# Y1-02 Problem Set 2 — Storage Arbitrage and Investment Economics

## Problem 1 — One-day storage arbitrage

A battery has 10 MWh usable energy capacity, 5 MW charge/discharge power limits, 90% charging efficiency, and 90% discharging efficiency. It begins and ends the four-hour horizon empty. Prices are:

| Hour | Price ($/MWh) |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 20 |
| 2 | 30 |
| 3 | 80 |
| 4 | 100 |

Assume no degradation cost, no ancillary-service revenue, no network constraints, and no uncertainty.

1. Formulate the linear optimization problem using charge, discharge, and state of charge.
2. Determine an optimal schedule.
3. Calculate energy purchased, energy delivered, and gross arbitrage margin.
4. Explain why round-trip efficiency is the product of charging and discharging efficiencies.
5. Add a $15/MWh throughput degradation cost and recalculate whether the same schedule remains profitable.
6. Identify the real-world factors omitted from this deterministic price-taking model.

## Problem 2 — Levelized cost versus system value

Two generation options have different capacity factors, capital costs, operating costs, and dispatch profiles. Explain why a lower levelized cost of energy (LCOE) does not necessarily imply a lower system cost or higher system value. Discuss timing, location, firm capacity, transmission, balancing, curtailment, and financing assumptions.

## Problem 3 — Investment under uncertainty

A project requires $50 million today and produces expected net cash flows of $8 million per year for 10 years. Use a 7% discount rate.

1. Calculate NPV.
2. Recalculate at 5% and 10%.
3. Explain how construction delay, cost overrun, price uncertainty, and policy risk affect the decision.
4. Explain when a real-options framework may be more informative than a single expected-NPV estimate.

## Submission

Include a reproducible spreadsheet or script, equations, sensitivity table, and a 750–1,000 word interpretation. Separate gross arbitrage revenue from net project value.
