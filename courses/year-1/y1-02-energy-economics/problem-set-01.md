# Y1-02 Problem Set 1 — Demand, Welfare, and Externalities

**Instructions:** Show derivations, units, and assumptions. Distinguish private costs from social costs. Unless stated otherwise, use linear inverse demand and constant marginal cost.

## Problem 1 — Demand and elasticity

A small industrial customer has hourly inverse demand:

    P(Q) = 120 - 0.02Q

where P is $/MWh and Q is MWh in the hour.

1. Calculate quantity demanded at prices of $40, $60, and $80/MWh.
2. Derive point price elasticity of demand and calculate it at P=$60/MWh.
3. Explain why the elasticity varies along a linear demand curve.
4. If a tariff adds a fixed monthly charge but leaves the marginal energy price unchanged, explain why the short-run quantity response may differ from a volumetric price increase.

## Problem 2 — Competitive equilibrium and welfare

Market demand is:

    P_D(Q) = 100 - 0.1Q

Competitive supply is:

    P_S(Q) = 20 + 0.05Q

1. Solve for equilibrium price and quantity.
2. Calculate consumer surplus and producer surplus.
3. Calculate total surplus.
4. Impose a price ceiling of $40/MWh. Calculate quantity demanded and supplied at that price and the shortage.
5. Explain why the simple static model does not fully describe an electricity market with real-time balancing and network constraints.

## Problem 3 — Externality and carbon pricing

A generator's private marginal cost is $35/MWh and its emissions rate is 0.55 tCO2/MWh. Assume, for this exercise only, a constant social cost of carbon of $80/tCO2.

1. Calculate the external damage per MWh.
2. Calculate social marginal cost.
3. What carbon price would internalize the assumed externality?
4. Explain how dispatch changes if a second generator has private marginal cost $55/MWh and emissions rate 0.10 tCO2/MWh.
5. Identify at least four reasons actual marginal emissions and damages may not be constant.

## Problem 4 — Long-run versus short-run cost

A utility is evaluating a $100 million substation project with an expected 40-year life. Its annual fixed O&M is $1.5 million, and expected annual variable operating cost is $0.5 million. Assume a real discount rate of 5% and end-of-year costs.

1. Calculate the present value of the 40-year annual O&M stream.
2. Calculate total present cost including initial capital.
3. Explain why dividing this present cost by forecast energy throughput is not automatically a valid estimate of the marginal price of electricity.
4. List major uncertainties that should be tested.

Submit a derivation-based solution, one sensitivity table, and a 500–800 word interpretation. State clearly where the exercise abstracts from actual tariff, market, and regulatory rules.
