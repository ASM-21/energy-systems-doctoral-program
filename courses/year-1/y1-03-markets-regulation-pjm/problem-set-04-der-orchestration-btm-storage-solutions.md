# Solution Notes — DER Orchestration, Cost Recovery, and Non-Exporting BTM Storage

## Problem 1 — Chronology and source hierarchy

The December 3, 2025 order concerns PPL's Second DER Management Plan in P-2024-3049223. The March 13, 2026 joint petition is a settlement filing in the separate PPL base-rate case, R-2025-3057164. The PUC entered its order approving that settlement on June 11, 2026 with a minor modification related to net-metering eligibility. New rates became effective July 1, 2026. PPL's Q3 2026 Form 10-Q reports that reconsideration was denied August 27, 2026 and a limited net-metering appeal was filed September 25, 2026.

The settlement appendix includes proposed findings of fact. Statements in those proposed findings summarize party positions and evidence; they are not all adjudicated facts. In particular, the $1,700/device figure is attributed to Environmental Intervenors' testimony. It must not be represented as a Commission-verified cost.

## Problem 2 — DER orchestration

Paragraph 118 requires one stakeholder working group before filing the DER Orchestration Plan, with substantive updates, questions, feedback, and good-faith consideration of stakeholder feedback. It does not itself specify the plan's technical architecture, mandate adoption of stakeholder recommendations, or establish a quantified savings target.

A defensible cost model must separate capital and operating components and distinguish per-device costs from shared platform costs. Benefits should be measured against a baseline or counterfactual, such as avoided or deferred distribution upgrades, reductions in voltage excursions, lower curtailment, improved DER utilization, or measurable reductions in peak loading. The projected up-to-258 MW hosting-capacity figure is not evidence that 258 MW of new DER was actually interconnected or that the capacity was uniformly available across feeders.

## Problem 3 — Non-exporting BTM storage

Paragraph 123 is limited to BTM non-exporting battery projects already under interconnection review at the time of the settlement. It requires a review of whether the requirements being imposed align with industry best practices and available studies. It does not expressly require a new tariff, a complete rulemaking, or approval of every pending project.

Counting 120 calendar days from June 11, 2026 yields October 9, 2026. The date is a straightforward calendar calculation, not proof of filing, completion, or compliance. The docket and any controlling time-computation rule should be checked, as should any public record of the review's outcome.

Non-exporting storage is not automatically equivalent to a generating resource that exports energy. A paired solar-plus-storage system may have generation export rights while storage export remains restricted. An exporting storage resource raises distinct interconnection, metering, market participation, and compensation questions. A sound non-export design should specify inverter operating modes, export-limit controls, protective functions, meter location and resolution, fail-safe response to loss of communications, commissioning tests, and utility verification rights.

## Problem 4 — Synthesis

A strong memo should avoid two opposite errors: assuming that non-exporting storage has no grid impact, or assuming that it creates the same risks as an exporting generator. Charging can increase local load and alter voltage/current profiles; discharging can reduce load and, if controls fail or are misconfigured, can create export. Requirements should therefore be tied to the actual configuration and credible failure modes.

The memo should distinguish a one-time review obligation from a durable rulemaking. It should recommend transparent criteria, consistent application, documented technical rationale, clear customer responsibilities, and a mechanism to update requirements as inverter standards and field evidence evolve. It should also identify what public evidence is still missing: the paragraph 123 review record, any resulting policy revision, the DER Orchestration Plan, stakeholder comments, and measured program outcomes.
