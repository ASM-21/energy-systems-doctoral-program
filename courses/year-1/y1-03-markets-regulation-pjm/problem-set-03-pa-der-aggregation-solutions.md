# Solution Notes: Pennsylvania DER Aggregation and Regulatory Boundaries

## Problem 1 — Decision classification

1. Physical distribution interconnection is governed by the applicable state/utility interconnection framework, including PPL Rule 12 and Pennsylvania PUC oversight.
2. Approval for a component DER to participate in a DERA aggregation is a separate EDC review contemplated by proposed 52 Pa. Code § 57.264 and the PJM tariff framework.
3. PJM determines wholesale registration and market eligibility under its tariff, Operating Agreement, and manuals, subject to FERC oversight.
4. The EDC evaluates distribution-system effects and safe/reliable operation under state-regulated distribution authority and applicable tariffs/rules.
5. Distribution override procedures are contemplated in the proposed Chapter 57 framework and must be reconciled with PJM dispatch and the specific FERC/PJM rules.

The physical interconnection approval is not the same as wholesale aggregation participation approval. The NOPR expressly says that Component DER interconnection will be addressed in another proceeding.

## Problem 2 — Net-metering and wholesale compensation

The proposed NOPR generally excludes customer-generators taking service under an EDC net-metering tariff from wholesale energy and capacity participation as a DER Aggregation Resource. It proposes allowing ancillary-services participation because net-metering compensation does not itself pay for real-time dispatch of ancillary services. The proposed release valve could allow energy/capacity participation where the EDC confirms that the retail tariff does not create duplicative compensation and PJM separately determines that other market requirements are met.

These are proposed conditions, not final rules. The policy problem is avoiding payment twice for the same service while not blocking distinct services that a DER can provide.

## Problem 3 — Data access

The NOPR proposes that EDCs provide DERA access to Component DER data upon consent of the DER operator, in a format approved by the Commission. It does not make such data publicly available. Consent is central because historical usage, customer load, and operating information can be sensitive. A final framework should specify data minimization, purpose limitation, retention, cybersecurity, breach notification, role-based access, audit logs, and revocation/renewal of consent.

## Problem 4 — Review timeline and fees

The proposed 60-day review period applies to the EDC process for requests to approve a Component DER's participation as a DERA resource. It is not a general interconnection study deadline. An interconnection impact study evaluates the physical connection and system upgrades; DERA participation review considers wholesale market operation and its distribution-system implications. A fee should be transparent, cost-based, non-discriminatory, and proportionate to actual review effort. The final rules should define the start/stop clock, completeness criteria, requests for information, and denial reasons.

## Problem 5 — Policy memo framework

A strong memo will:
- distinguish distribution interconnection from wholesale participation;
- treat the proposed rulemaking as pending, not enacted;
- assess the proposed net-metering exclusion and release valve as an anti-double-compensation design;
- identify customer consent and data governance as prerequisites for scalable participation;
- evaluate whether the 60-day review, fee provisions, and denial explanations create predictability;
- explain EDC override authority as a reliability safeguard and analyze the risk of excessive or opaque curtailment;
- assess whether the proposal allocates administrative and upgrade costs fairly;
- separately discuss PPL's DERMS/Rule 12 requirements and its Second DER Management Plan, avoiding the claim that either is the Chapter 57 rulemaking.

A strong answer also presents counterarguments: overly broad exclusions may prevent legitimate market participation, while weak anti-double-compensation controls may shift costs to nonparticipants. Any final recommendation should be conditional on the final regulation, PJM tariff, and implementation evidence.
