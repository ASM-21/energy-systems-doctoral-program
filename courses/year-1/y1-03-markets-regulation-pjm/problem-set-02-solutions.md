# Y1-03 Problem Set 2 — Solution Notes

## Problem 1

Weighted cost of capital:

    WACC = (200/500)*5% + (300/500)*9% = 7.4%

Allowed return on rate base = $500m*7.4% = $37m.

Simplified revenue requirement:

    37 + 10 + 25 + 5 - 2 = $75 million/year

This is a revenue requirement, not a direct volumetric energy price. Retail tariffs allocate costs across customer classes and billing determinants, while wholesale energy costs and other charges may be treated separately. Regulatory review determines prudence, used-and-useful status, capital structure, allowed return, depreciation, taxes, and cost allocation.

## Problem 2

A fixed charge is stable but weakly tied to usage and can burden low-use customers. A volumetric charge creates an energy-conservation signal but may not recover peak-driven network costs and can create revenue erosion as usage falls. A demand charge can better reflect capacity contribution if the billing determinant aligns with system cost drivers, but customer peaks may not coincide with system peaks and measurement can be complex. Time-of-use rates can signal temporal system costs but require interval metering, customer comprehension, and careful period design.

## Problem 3

Possible methods include direct assignment to the triggering customer, beneficiary-pays allocation across beneficiaries, and a broader postage-stamp or socialized allocation. Hybrid approaches may separate incremental facilities from shared backbone upgrades. Each approach makes different assumptions about causation, option value, future beneficiaries, and stranded-asset risk. The engineering study identifies constraints and necessary facilities; regulators determine how costs are assigned under governing law and tariff rules.
