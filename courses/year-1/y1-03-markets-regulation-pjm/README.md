# Y1-03 — Electricity Markets, Utility Regulation, and PJM

**Status:** Active Year 1 course  
**Level:** Graduate / applied institutional analysis  
**Prerequisites:** Y1-01 and Y1-02, or equivalent power-system and microeconomic literacy; basic quantitative methods. Y1-04 may be taken before or alongside this course.  
**Planned effort:** **51 hours** (40–52 hour planning range), normally 10–17 weeks at 3–5 hours per week.
**Position in sequence:** Integrative Year 1 course. Applies engineering and economics to real institutions, rules, and infrastructure decisions.  
**Last content review:** 2026-11-23; recheck all cited manual revisions before future use.

## Purpose

Understand the institutions and rules governing U.S. wholesale electricity markets, regulated utilities, and DER participation. PJM and Pennsylvania provide recurring applied cases, but their rules should not be generalized to other regions without comparison.

The course emphasizes source discipline: distinguish statutes, regulations, commission orders, tariffs, market manuals, informational presentations, party testimony, and independent analysis.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the learner should be able to:

1. Explain the respective roles of FERC, state commissions, RTOs/ISOs, NERC, utilities, market monitors, and customers.
2. Distinguish wholesale energy, capacity/resource adequacy, ancillary services, transmission rights, and retail distribution service.
3. Explain day-ahead and real-time market clearing, locational marginal prices, congestion, and losses at an introductory analytical level.
4. Interpret utility revenue requirements, rate design, and cost allocation without conflating accounting recovery with economic efficiency.
5. Identify federal/state jurisdictional boundaries and the legal status of primary documents.
6. Analyze how interconnection, transmission planning, DER aggregation, and distribution constraints shape investment and participation.
7. Reproduce simplified market-clearing and co-optimization calculations and interpret dual values.
8. Produce a source-supported regulatory or market analysis with precise dates, versions, scope, and limitations.

## Core and primary materials

- FERC, *Wholesale Electricity Markets: Overview and Guide*: [official guide](https://www.ferc.gov/wholesale-electricity-markets-overview-and-guide).
- PJM manuals and tariffs: [official library](https://www.pjm.com/library/manuals); PJM data: [Data Miner](https://dataminer2.pjm.com/).
- PJM market-monitor reports: [Monitoring Analytics](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/).
- FERC eLibrary: [official docket repository](https://elibrary.ferc.gov/).
- Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission: [official website](https://www.puc.pa.gov/).
- NERC reliability publications: [official resource library](https://www.nerc.com/pa/RAPA/ra/Pages/default.aspx).

For capacity and resource-adequacy assignments, use the manual revision and effective date specified in the assignment. At the time this course was last reviewed, the relevant references included PJM Manual 18 Rev. 63 (effective 2026-09-24), Manual 20A Rev. 3 (effective 2026-06-24), and Manual 21B Rev. 06 (effective 2026-11-18). Check the official manual archive before relying on those revisions in later work. Manuals 20 and 21A were retired effective 2025-06-01 in the cited course research.

## Assessment structure

- Problem sets and analytical exercises: 35%.
- Computational market models: 12%.
- Primary-source regulatory/manual analysis: 13%.
- Final report and policy brief: 40%.

Use the [program rubric](../../../ACADEMIC_STANDARDS.md). These are internal self-assessment weights, not university grades.

## Final project

Select a focused topic such as PJM interconnection and cost allocation, capacity/resource adequacy, DER aggregation, data-center load growth, transmission expansion, or Pennsylvania utility investment incentives. Produce:
- a 2,000–3,000 word report;
- a one-page policy brief;
- an institutional/jurisdiction map;
- a source register with exact document title, section, docket/manual revision, effective date, URL, and access date;
- reproducible calculations or a documented analytical framework;
- explicit treatment of uncertainty, distributional consequences, and limits.

Do not treat a press release as a controlling authority. Do not describe a proposal, settlement petition, informational presentation, or party testimony as an effective rule or commission finding unless the operative order establishes that status.

## Completion gate

Attempt and correct all six problem sets; complete both computational labs and the source exercise; submit the final report and brief; and complete a self-assessment against the [program rubric](../../../ACADEMIC_STANDARDS.md). Record actual hours and evidence. Do not use confidential employer or customer information.
