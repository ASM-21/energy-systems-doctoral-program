# Y1-03 Problem Set 4 — DER Orchestration, Cost Recovery, and Non-Exporting BTM Storage

**Estimated time:** 4–5 hours  
**Casebook:** [Pennsylvania DER interconnection and PJM wholesale aggregation](../../../research/casebooks/pa-der-interconnection/README.md)  
**Primary sources:** PPL 2025 rate-case settlement in R-2025-3057164; PPL Q3 2026 Form 10-Q; PPL Energy Storage Interconnection Policy and Guidance; current PPL Rule 12.

## Problem 1 — Regulatory chronology and source hierarchy

Construct a chronology for the Second DER Management Plan and the 2025 PPL base-rate case. Include the December 3, 2025 DER Management Plan order, the March 13, 2026 non-unanimous rate-case settlement filing, the June 11, 2026 order approving the settlement with a minor net-metering modification, the July 1, 2026 new-rate effective date, the August 27, 2026 denial of reconsideration, and the September 25, 2026 limited appeal.

Classify each source as a Commission order, approved settlement term, proposed finding, party testimony, utility technical guidance, or issuer disclosure. Explain why a proposed finding in a settlement appendix is not automatically a Commission finding.

## Problem 2 — DER orchestration plan and cost-effectiveness

Settlement body paragraph 119 provides for a stakeholder working group before PPL files the DER Orchestration Plan. Appendix D proposed findings 386–390 summarize Environmental Intervenors' arguments that the plan could reduce the Second DER Management Plan's cost and cite $1,700 per device; PPL's rebuttal disputed the timing and cost-reduction implications. Note that Appendix D cross-references the operative stakeholder commitment as Settlement paragraph 118, while the settlement body labels it paragraph 119.

1. Explain what the stakeholder working group provision requires and what it does not require.
2. Identify why the $1,700/device figure cannot be used as an established actual cost without examining its source testimony and exhibits.
3. Build a lifecycle cost taxonomy covering device hardware, installation, communications, software, cybersecurity, maintenance, replacement, and program administration.
4. Propose at least four measurable benefits and define an appropriate counterfactual for each.
5. Explain why projected hosting-capacity increase, installed device count, and realized hosting capacity are distinct quantities.

## Problem 3 — Non-exporting BTM battery storage

Settlement paragraph 124 requires PPL to review, within 120 days of the final order, BTM non-exporting battery projects then under interconnection review and assess whether imposed requirements align with industry best practices and available studies. The settlement records Convergent's position that PPL lacked a distinct BTM non-exporting BESS rule and PPL's response that its Energy Storage Interconnection Policy and Guidance provided clear technical standards.

1. Identify the exact scope of the paragraph 124 review. What projects are included, and what is the stated review criterion?
2. Calculate the 120-calendar-day date from June 11, 2026. Explain why this is a target derived from the settlement and why the docket/order should be checked for the applicable time-computation rule.
3. Compare non-exporting BTM storage, storage paired with net-metered generation, and an exporting storage resource. Explain why the interconnection and compensation questions differ.
4. Specify the control, protection, metering, commissioning, and fail-safe evidence needed to substantiate non-export operation.
5. Distinguish a policy review from adoption of a new tariff or a new interconnection rule.

## Problem 4 — Policy and engineering synthesis

Write a 1,000–1,500 word memo assessing whether the regulatory framework creates a proportionate and transparent pathway for non-exporting storage and active DER management. Address:
- safety and reliability risk from inadvertent export;
- cost causation and the risk of applying generator requirements to a non-exporting storage configuration;
- consistency with IEEE 1547 and applicable inverter/interconnection standards;
- DERMS device cost, lifecycle cost, and alternatives;
- the value and limits of stakeholder review;
- what evidence the PUC would need to assess whether the program produces measurable benefits.

## Deliverables

- Docket chronology with status labels and citations.
- Cost taxonomy and benefit/counterfactual matrix.
- Technical checklist for non-export verification.
- Policy memo with explicit evidence gaps.
