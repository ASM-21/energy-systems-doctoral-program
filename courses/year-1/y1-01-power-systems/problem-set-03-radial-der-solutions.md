# Solution Notes: Radial Feeder DER Integration

These are benchmark checks, not a substitute for showing the calculations. Small differences may result from rounding.

## Problem 1 — Baseline branch quantities

The branch flows equal the sum of downstream loads:

| Branch | P (kW) | Q (kvar) | S (MVA) | Limit (MVA) | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1–2 | 400 | 160 | 0.431 | 1.50 | Within limit |
| 2–3 | 120 | 50 | 0.130 | 0.80 | Within limit |
| 2–4 | 100 | 40 | 0.108 | 0.80 | Within limit |

For each branch, (S=\sqrt{P^2+Q^2}/1000) MVA.

## Problem 2 — Approximate voltage profile

At 12.47 kV, the squared voltage denominator in the stated approximation is (12.47^2=155.5009;kV^2).

- Bus 2: (1-(0.70(0.400)+0.45(0.160))/155.5009 \approx 0.997737;pu).
- Bus 3: subtract an additional ((0.80(0.120)+0.50(0.050))/155.5009\approx0.000778;pu), giving approximately 0.996959 pu.
- Bus 4: subtract an additional ((1.20(0.100)+0.65(0.040))/155.5009\approx0.000939;pu), giving approximately 0.996798 pu.

The root bus is the fixed-voltage reference. Since it is included in the voltage dictionary at exactly 1.0 pu, the maximum across all buses is 1.0 pu in the baseline case. The minimum is the most remote/lowest-voltage bus.

## Problem 3 — DER penetration and reverse flow

Branch 2–4 serves the 100 kW load at bus 4. It reverses when PV exceeds 100 kW. Branch 1–2 serves the total 400 kW feeder load, so it reverses when bus-4 PV exceeds 400 kW. Equality gives zero real-power flow, not reverse flow. Reactive demand remains positive because the model assumes unity-power-factor PV.

## Problem 4 — Binding screening constraint

At 800 kW PV, branch 2–4 real flow is -700 kW and reactive flow is +40 kvar:
(S=\sqrt{700^2+40^2}/1000\approx0.701\;MVA), below 0.80 MVA.

At 900 kW PV, real flow is -800 kW and reactive flow is +40 kvar:
(S=\sqrt{800^2+40^2}/1000\approx0.801\;MVA), slightly above the 0.80 MVA limit. The first discrete sweep violation is therefore 900 kW for a 100 kW step.

This is only the first violation under the synthetic data, static snapshot, approximate model, and selected rating. It is not a feeder hosting-capacity value. A real study requires validated circuit models, multiple operating conditions, protection and equipment criteria, inverter controls, and utility-specific procedures.

## Problem 5 — Voltage and thermal constraints

The 2–4 thermal limit binds first in this case, at 900 kW on the 100 kW sweep. A higher PV level eventually creates an overvoltage in the approximate model; the exact first discrete level should be read from the supplied code output for the specified step size. Voltage and thermal limits are separate tests and need not bind at the same penetration.

Other real-world constraints include protection coordination/anti-islanding, regulator and capacitor operation, phase imbalance, transformer loading, flicker/power quality, fault-current contribution, and operational limits on inverter settings.

## Problem 6 — Sensitivity and model criticism

Moving the DER closer to the root generally reduces the voltage rise and may reduce reverse flow on upstream branches for a given DER size, but it changes which branches carry reverse flow and may alter the first thermal constraint. Increasing resistance increases voltage sensitivity to real-power flow. Increasing load generally reduces voltage rise and delays reverse flow for a fixed PV size, while increasing forward thermal loading.

The exact first-violation values should be generated by rerunning the code, not inferred from these qualitative statements.

At least five limitations:
1. Balanced three-phase approximation; no phase-specific loading.
2. Constant-P/Q loads; no voltage-dependent demand.
3. No losses in downstream aggregation.
4. No nonlinear AC power-flow solution.
5. No regulator, capacitor, or inverter Volt/VAR control.
6. No protection, fault-current, anti-islanding, or relay-coordination checks.
7. Static snapshot rather than time-series coincidence.
8. Simple apparent-power rating, not conductor ampacity under actual installation/weather.
9. No uncertainty, contingencies, or equipment-specific operating logic.

Validation plan: reproduce a published IEEE distribution test feeder; document the base case and units; compare bus voltages and branch flows against an AC-capable solver such as OpenDSS or pandapower; quantify error across load/PV snapshots; then add time-series load and PV profiles and explicit voltage, thermal, protection, and operational criteria.
