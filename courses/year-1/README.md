# Year 1 — Integrated Foundations

**Status:** Active foundation sequence  
**Total planned effort:** **168 course hours**, plus a 25-hour integration project (193 hours at the current planning baseline). Individual course ranges allow for prerequisite gaps and variable depth.  
**Pace:** 3–5 hours per week across the program, not per course. Individual courses typically span about 7–17 weeks at that pace; the full sequence and integration project take roughly 38–66 weeks. Extend the calendar rather than skipping required work.

## Purpose

Year 1 establishes four connected capabilities: understand the physical electricity system, reason about energy economics, analyze public data reproducibly, and interpret market and regulatory institutions. The courses are designed to converge on one applied research project rather than four unrelated exercises.

## How to use each course

Each course guide contains its outcomes, prerequisites, learning sequence, core materials, required assignments, computational work, final deliverable, completion gate, and next step. Use the linked solution only after attempting the assignment. Preserve corrected derivations and code as evidence.

The planned hours reconcile to the progress/course-tracker.csv and progress/year-1-assessment-tracker.csv files. Record actual time and evidence there. The ranges shown in individual course guides are realistic allowances, not additional hours to add on top of the planning baseline.

## Year 1 integration project

Select one focused question involving distribution infrastructure, DER interconnection, utility incentives, PJM markets, load growth, or related energy-policy issues. Reuse methods and evidence from the four courses.

Minimum deliverables:
1. A narrow research question, motivation, and scope.
2. A 2,000–3,000 word literature and institutional review.
3. A data dictionary or transparent model specification with source provenance and exclusions.
4. A reproducible engineering or empirical analysis.
5. Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis.
6. A 1,000–1,500 word policy or technical brief.
7. Reproduction instructions, presentation, and self-assessment.

Public aggregate data do not automatically support application-level, feeder-level, or causal conclusions. Complete a data-feasibility assessment before committing to an identification strategy. Do not use confidential employer, customer, or operational information.

## Year 1 progression gate

Before entering the planned Year 2 sequence, demonstrate that you can:
- represent a simplified power system and explain model assumptions;
- solve and interpret dispatch, marginal cost, welfare, and storage problems;
- distinguish the roles of FERC, state commissions, utilities, and PJM using primary sources;
- acquire and audit public data, reproduce an analysis, and communicate uncertainty;
- formulate a research question and explain what evidence would and would not identify its answer.

Use the [curriculum map](../../curriculum/course-map.md) and [academic standards](../../ACADEMIC_STANDARDS.md) to review the evidence. A course page, completed reading list, or passing software test is not by itself proof of mastery.
