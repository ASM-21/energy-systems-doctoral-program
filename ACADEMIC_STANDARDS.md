# Academic Standards and Assessment Policy

## Standard
The program aims at graduate and early-doctoral intellectual rigor. It does not simulate an accredited university degree: there is no institutional award, external examiner, formal advisor, or academic credit. Mastery claims must be supported by completed work.

A strong submission demonstrates conceptual accuracy, defensible mathematics, appropriate methods, traceable evidence, reproducible computation, and interpretation that distinguishes correlation from causation and engineering feasibility from policy desirability.

## Course design and learner experience
Every course page must use the shared [course template](curriculum/course-template.md) as its structural baseline and clearly state status, level, prerequisites, expected effort, learning outcomes, sequence, required and optional work, assessments, solution links, computational resources, final deliverables, and the next progression step. Keep course-specific depth, but do not omit learner-facing orientation.

Problem sets must link to corresponding worked solutions where available. Label solution notes honestly (full derivation, worked check, or instructor notes). Learners should be instructed to attempt assignments before consulting solutions. Distinguish required core work from optional enrichment and reference material.

## Required learning evidence
Each course should produce:
1. A structured concept map or synthesis.
2. At least two substantive problem sets.
3. One computational or empirical exercise.
4. One written analytical memo.
5. A final project with reproducible artifacts.
6. A self-assessment identifying remaining weaknesses.

Reading or watching material is not completion evidence.

## Internal assessment rubric
Score each dimension 0–4.

| Dimension | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conceptual accuracy | Incorrect/absent | Mostly correct; gaps | Precise and connected |
| Technical rigor | Unsupported | Method applied with limited justification | Derivation, assumptions, checks sound |
| Evidence | Unsupported claims | Relevant but incomplete sources | Primary evidence and traceable citations |
| Reproducibility | Not reproducible | Partial code/data notes | End-to-end reproducible and validated |
| Interpretation | Restates output | Some implications | Uncertainty, trade-offs, alternatives |
| Communication | Unclear | Understandable | Concise and decision-useful |

A satisfactory final project requires at least 18/24, with no zero in technical rigor, evidence, or reproducibility. 21/24 or higher indicates strong work. These are internal standards, not university grades.

## Research integrity
- Never fabricate sources, quotations, results, datasets, or peer-review status.
- Cite the source actually consulted; independently verify citations.
- Label substantial AI assistance in drafting, code generation, or analysis.
- Verify AI-generated calculations, code, and factual claims.
- Preserve raw data separately from cleaned and derived data.
- Record exclusions, transformations, missingness, and sensitivity tests.
- Do not present exploratory correlations as causal estimates.
- Do not disclose confidential employer, customer, grid, or personal data.
- Respect data, software, and publication licenses.

## Source hierarchy
Prefer statutes, regulations, FERC/state commission orders, tariffs, market manuals, NERC standards and reports; then peer-reviewed research and university materials; then DOE, EIA, NREL, LBNL, CRS, National Academies and RTO/ISO data; then reputable industry analysis and journalism for context.

## Doctoral-style research expectations
A research contribution states a narrow question, importance, prior knowledge, conceptual framework, data and method, assumptions and threats to validity, results, robustness checks, implications, limits, and next steps. Novelty is expected for work labeled a research contribution, not every course assignment.

## Workload
Default weekly allocation: 1–1.5 hours textbook/lecture, 1 hour primary source or paper, 1–1.5 hours problem set/coding, 0.5–1 hour synthesis and research log. Reduce scope rather than standards when time is constrained.
