# Energy Systems, Infrastructure Economics & Public Policy

An independent, doctoral-level learning and research program spanning power systems engineering, energy economics, electricity markets, regulation, quantitative methods, climate policy, and infrastructure strategy.

!!! warning "Independent study, not an accredited degree"
    This curriculum is independently designed. It does not confer university credit, a degree, professional licensure, or institutional affiliation. Completion is evidenced by work products, not attendance or reading alone.

## The program

The objective is to understand energy infrastructure as a coupled technical, economic, regulatory, and strategic system. The initial focus is the U.S. electricity sector, with PJM and Pennsylvania as recurring cases.

**Designed workload:** 3–5 hours per week.  
**Initial horizon:** Four years, followed by specialization and original research.

## The six departments

Power systems engineering; energy economics and electricity markets; regulation and public policy; climate and sustainability; quantitative and computational methods; infrastructure strategy and national competitiveness.

## Research direction

The initial flagship research candidate examines distribution infrastructure, DER interconnection, and deployment outcomes in Pennsylvania. It begins with institutional mapping and a data-feasibility review; it does not assume public data can support causal claims.

See the [research proposal](../projects/policy-analysis/pjm-distribution-der-research-proposal.md).

## Academic standard

Every major course should produce problem-set solutions, computational work, written analysis, and a reproducible final project. Research claims require traceable sources, explicit assumptions, uncertainty analysis, and limitations.

The repository is the canonical record. See [README](../README.md) for the full project description.
