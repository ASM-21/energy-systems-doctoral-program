# Energy Systems, Infrastructure Economics & Public Policy

An independent, research-oriented doctoral-level learning program focused on the engineering, economics, regulation, and strategic development of energy infrastructure.

**Status:** Independent self-study curriculum. This is not an accredited degree, university affiliation, professional credential, or substitute for supervised doctoral research.

## Purpose
Build the ability to understand, model, evaluate, and influence energy systems as coupled technical, economic, institutional, and geopolitical infrastructure. The U.S. electricity sector is the primary empirical setting, with international comparisons where useful.

The learner enters with graduate mechanical-engineering preparation, economics exposure, quantitative research experience, and utility-sector exposure. The curriculum therefore emphasizes power-system literacy, electricity-market economics, regulatory analysis, applied econometrics, optimization, and independent research rather than repeating a conventional undergraduate engineering sequence.

## Program at a glance

**[Open the published academic site](https://asm-21.github.io/energy-systems-doctoral-program/)**
- Workload: 3–5 hours/week; approximately 150–225 hours/year.
- Horizon: Four-year initial sequence, followed by open-ended specialization and research.
- Standard: Graduate/doctoral-level intellectual rigor, adapted for part-time independent study.
- Outputs: Reproducible computational projects, technical reports, policy briefs, literature reviews, working papers, and conference-quality presentations.
- Geographic focus: United States, with recurring attention to PJM and Pennsylvania.

## Quality principles
- No invented course numbers, syllabi, citations, datasets, or findings.
- Distinguish official source material, secondary commentary, and original analysis.
- Quantitative results must be reproducible from documented inputs and code.
- Policy recommendations must identify assumptions, distributional effects, uncertainty, and implementation constraints.
- Verify source links and effective dates before relying on them.
- Favor depth and durable understanding over reading volume.
