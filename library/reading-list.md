# Core Reading List

**Last bibliographic verification:** 2026-11-20. Links point to publishers or original journal records where possible. Availability, prices, and edition status can change; check a library before purchasing.

## Power systems and electricity economics

1. Daniel S. Kirschen and Goran Strbac, *Fundamentals of Power System Economics*, 2nd ed., Wiley, 2018. ISBN 978-1-119-21324-6 (print). [Wiley publisher page](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/fundamentals-of-power-system-economics-2nd-edition-p-9781119213246). Graduate-level treatment connecting power-system operation and planning with market economics; useful as a core text for Y1-01/Y1-02 and later market-design work.
2. Steven Stoft, *Power System Economics: Designing Markets for Electricity*, Wiley-IEEE Press, 2002. ISBN 978-0-471-15040-4. [Wiley publisher page](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/power-system-economics-designing-markets-for-electricity-p-9780471150404). A foundational market-design text; some institutional examples predate current market rules, so pair conceptual arguments with current tariffs and manuals.
3. Paul L. Joskow, “Lessons Learned From Electricity Market Liberalization,” *The Energy Journal*, 29(2_suppl), 9–42 (2008). DOI: [10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol29-NoSI2-3](https://doi.org/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol29-NoSI2-3). [Journal record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol29-NoSI2-3). Use as a framework for evaluating reform outcomes; do not treat 2008 analysis as a description of today's rules.

## Econometrics and causal inference

4. Jeffrey M. Wooldridge, *Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach*, 8th ed., Cengage, 2025. ISBN 978-0-357-90016-1 (print). [Cengage publisher page](https://www.cengage.com/c/introductory-econometrics-a-modern-approach-8e-wooldridge/9780357900161). Current edition verified on publisher page. Use selected chapters for regression, panel data, and treatment-effect foundations; the program's Year 2 econometrics sequence must go beyond an introductory text.
5. Joshua D. Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke, *Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion*, Princeton University Press, 2009. [Publisher page](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691120355/mostly-harmless-econometrics). A methods reference for applied identification and empirical design.
6. Scott Cunningham, *Causal Inference: The Mixtape*, Yale University Press, 2021. [Open online text](https://mixtape.scunning.com/). Applied guide to causal inference methods; use alongside formal econometric treatments and original papers.

## Data and institutional references

- [U.S. Energy Information Administration Open Data](https://www.eia.gov/opendata/) — U.S. energy statistics and series metadata.
- [FERC eLibrary](https://elibrary.ferc.gov/) — primary federal filings and orders.
- [PJM manuals and tariffs](https://www.pjm.com/library/manuals) — controlling RTO materials; check the current version and effective date.
- [Monitoring Analytics](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/) — PJM market-monitor reports and analysis.
- [NERC](https://www.nerc.com/) — reliability standards, assessments, and event reports.
- [LBNL Electricity Markets & Policy](https://emp.lbl.gov/) — research on utility regulation, DERs, interconnection, and electricity policy.
- [NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/) — renewable integration, grid, technology, and modeling research.

## Reading protocol

For every substantive paper, record: complete citation; research question; contribution; theory; data/sample; method and identifying assumptions; main findings; limitations; relevance; and follow-up papers. Distinguish peer-reviewed articles, working papers, government reports, market-monitor reports, and advocacy/industry commentary. Never cite a source you have not checked.
