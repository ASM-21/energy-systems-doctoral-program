# Core Reading List

**Last bibliographic verification:** 2026-11-22. Links point to publishers or original journal records where possible. Availability, prices, and edition status can change; check a library before purchasing.

## Power systems and electricity economics

1. Daniel S. Kirschen and Goran Strbac, *Fundamentals of Power System Economics*, 2nd ed., Wiley, 2018. ISBN 978-1-119-21324-6 (print). [Wiley publisher page](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/fundamentals-of-power-system-economics-2nd-edition-p-9781119213246). Graduate-level treatment connecting power-system operation and planning with market economics; useful as a core text for Y1-01/Y1-02 and later market-design work.
2. Steven Stoft, *Power System Economics: Designing Markets for Electricity*, Wiley-IEEE Press, 2002. ISBN 978-0-471-15040-4. [Wiley publisher page](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/power-system-economics-designing-markets-for-electricity-p-9780471150404). A foundational market-design text; some institutional examples predate current market rules, so pair conceptual arguments with current tariffs and manuals.
3. Paul L. Joskow, “Lessons Learned From Electricity Market Liberalization,” *The Energy Journal*, 29(2_suppl), 9–42 (2008). DOI: [10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol29-NoSI2-3](https://doi.org/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol29-NoSI2-3). [Journal record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol29-NoSI2-3). Use as a framework for evaluating reform outcomes; do not treat 2008 analysis as a description of today's rules.
4. Peter Cramton, “Electricity Market Design,” *Oxford Review of Economic Policy*, 33(4), 589–612 (2017). DOI: [10.1093/oxrep/grx041](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxrep/grx041). [Journal record](https://academic.oup.com/oxrep/article/33/4/589/4587939). A concise framework for the joint short-run and long-run efficiency objectives of market design; use it to structure a critique of energy, reserve, and capacity mechanisms.

## DER integration and interconnection

- U.S. Department of Energy Office of Electricity, *Flexible DER & EV Connections* (July 2024). [Official PDF](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-08/Flexible%20DER%20%20EV%20Connections%20July%202024.pdf). Defines dynamic operating envelopes, flexible interconnections, and flexible service connections; compares customer-controlled and utility-direct-controlled solutions and includes California and Massachusetts examples.
- U.S. Department of Energy Office of Electricity, [Distributed Resource Utilization](https://www.energy.gov/oe/distributed-resource-utilization). A curated source page for distribution grid codes, DER orchestration, operational coordination, and distribution services.
- California Public Utilities Commission, [Resolution E-5296 (March 21, 2024)](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M527/K981/527981713.PDF). Primary regulatory comparator for Limited Generation Profiles: 24-value annual schedules, certified power-control systems, a 10% buffer, curtailment conditions, and monitoring/reporting.

## Econometrics and causal inference

5. Jeffrey M. Wooldridge, *Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach*, 8th ed., Cengage, 2025. ISBN 978-0-357-90016-1 (print). [Cengage publisher page](https://www.cengage.com/c/introductory-econometrics-a-modern-approach-8e-wooldridge/9780357900161). Current edition verified on publisher page. Use selected chapters for regression, panel data, and treatment-effect foundations; the program's Year 2 econometrics sequence must go beyond an introductory text.
6. Joshua D. Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke, *Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion*, Princeton University Press, 2009. [Publisher page](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691120355/mostly-harmless-econometrics). A methods reference for applied identification and empirical design.
7. Scott Cunningham, *Causal Inference: The Mixtape*, Yale University Press, 2021. [Open online text](https://mixtape.scunning.com/). Applied guide to causal inference methods; use alongside formal econometric treatments and original papers.
8. Guido W. Imbens and Donald B. Rubin, *Causal Inference for Statistics, Social, and Biomedical Sciences: An Introduction*, Cambridge University Press, 2015. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/causal-inference/). Potential-outcomes framework, design-based inference, and randomized experiments.
9. Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins, *Causal Inference: What If*, 2020. [Free author-hosted text](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/miguel-hernan/causal-inference-book/). Target-trial thinking, observational causal inference, and longitudinal treatment strategies.
10. Brantly Callaway and Pedro H. C. Sant'Anna, “Difference-in-Differences with Multiple Time Periods,” *Journal of Econometrics*, 225(2), 200–230 (2021). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.12.001). Group-time average treatment effects under staggered adoption.
11. Liyang Sun and Sarah Abraham, “Estimating Dynamic Treatment Effects in Event Studies with Heterogeneous Treatment Effects,” *Journal of Econometrics*, 225(2), 175–199 (2021). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.09.006). Event-study estimators under heterogeneous effects and staggered treatment.
12. Guido W. Imbens and Thomas Lemieux, “Regression Discontinuity Designs: A Guide to Practice,” *Journal of Econometrics*, 142(2), 615–635 (2008). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2007.05.001). Design and diagnostics for regression discontinuity.
13. Alberto Abadie, “Using Synthetic Controls: Feasibility, Data Requirements, and Methodological Aspects,” *Journal of Economic Literature*, 59(2), 391–425 (2021). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20191450). Synthetic-control design and inference considerations.


## Advanced mathematical methods

- Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization* (Cambridge University Press, 2004). [Author-hosted textbook and materials](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/). Core reference for convex sets/functions, duality, and optimization applications.
- Dimitri P. Bertsekas, *Convex Optimization Theory* (Athena Scientific, 2009). A more theoretical companion for convex analysis and optimization.
- Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright, *Numerical Optimization*, 2nd ed. (Springer, 2006). [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-40065-5). Numerical algorithms, convergence, and practical optimization.
- Gilbert Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra*, 6th ed. (Wellesley-Cambridge Press, 2023). Linear algebra and geometric foundations.
- Sheldon M. Ross, *Introduction to Probability Models*, 12th ed. (Academic Press, 2019). Probability and stochastic-process foundations.
- Karl J. Åström and Richard M. Murray, *Feedback Systems: An Introduction for Scientists and Engineers*. [Free online text](https://fbsbook.org/). Dynamical systems and control.
- MIT OpenCourseWare, [6.7220J Nonlinear Optimization (Spring 2025)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-7220j-nonlinear-optimization-spring-2025/), graduate-level reference for optimality conditions, convex analysis, duality, and algorithms.
- MIT OpenCourseWare, [6.253 Convex Analysis and Optimization (Spring 2012)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-253-convex-analysis-and-optimization-spring-2012/), graduate-level proof-based reference for convexity, duality, and algorithmic theory.
- MIT OpenCourseWare, [15.094J Systems Optimization: Models and Computation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-094j-systems-optimization-models-and-computation-sma-5223-spring-2004/), applied reference for network, discrete, nonlinear, and stochastic optimization.
- Alexander Shapiro, Darinka Dentcheva, and Andrzej Ruszczyński, *Lectures on Stochastic Programming: Modeling and Theory*, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2014. [SIAM book record](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611973433). A rigorous treatment of stochastic programming, risk, and multistage decision models.
- Dimitris Bertsimas, David B. Brown, and Constantine Caramanis, “Theory and Applications of Robust Optimization,” *SIAM Review*, 53(3), 464–501 (2011). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/080734510). Survey of robust optimization theory, tractability, and applications, including adaptable decisions.
- Odd O. Aalen and Søren Johansen, “An Empirical Transition Matrix for Non-homogeneous Markov Chains Based on Censored Observations,” *Scandinavian Journal of Statistics*, 5, 141–150 (1978). [Journal record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615704). Foundational reference for the Aalen-Johansen estimator.
- Hein Putter, Marta Fiocco, and R. B. Geskus, “Tutorial in Biostatistics: Competing Risks and Multi-State Models,” *Statistics in Medicine*, 26(11), 2389–2430 (2007). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.2712). Practical treatment of cumulative incidence and multi-state event histories.
- Jason P. Fine and Robert J. Gray, “A Proportional Hazards Model for the Subdistribution of a Competing Risk,” *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 94, 496–509 (1999). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1999.10474144). Regression framework for subdistribution hazards.
- Donald Gross, John F. Shortle, James M. Thompson, and Carl M. Harris, *Fundamentals of Queueing Theory*, 5th ed., Wiley, 2018. [Publisher record](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Fundamentals+of+Queueing+Theory%2C+5th+Edition-p-9781118943526). Reference for birth-death processes, Erlang formulas, queueing networks, and applied queueing analysis.
- Wallace J. Hopp and Mark L. Spearman, *Factory Physics*, 3rd ed., Waveland Press, 2011. Reference for process flow, variability, capacity, work-in-process, and the operational consequences of utilization.

Use these sources in the planned [Year 2 advanced mathematics course](../courses/year-2/advanced-mathematics-energy-systems/README.md) and [graduate econometrics and causal inference course](../courses/year-2/graduate-econometrics-causal-inference/README.md). They are a staged reference list, not a requirement to read every text cover to cover.

- [NERC](https://www.nerc.com/) — reliability standards, assessments, and event reports.

- [NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/) — renewable integration, grid, technology, and modeling research.

## Reading protocol

For every substantive paper, record: complete citation; research question; contribution; theory; data/sample; method and identifying assumptions; main findings; limitations; relevance; and follow-up papers. Distinguish peer-reviewed articles, working papers, government reports, market-monitor reports, and advocacy/industry commentary. Never cite a source you have not checked.
