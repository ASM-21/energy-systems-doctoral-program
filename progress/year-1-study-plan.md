# Year 1 Study Plan

## Time budget

The four core courses contain approximately 155 planned hours before the new Y1-03 advanced market-design assignment. Add 6 planned hours for scarcity pricing, resource adequacy, and ancillary-service co-optimization, bringing the core sequence to approximately 161 hours. The current applied extensions add approximately 36 hours: radial-feeder DER screening (8 h), IEEE 13-node OpenDSS benchmark (6 h), flexible interconnection and dynamic export-envelope optimization (5 h), carbon pricing/flexible demand and Pennsylvania DER aggregation (8 h), non-exporting BTM storage modeling (4 h), and DER orchestration/BTM storage policy analysis (5 h). The full instructional sequence is therefore about 197 hours before the integration project. At 3 hours/week, the core sequence takes about 54 weeks and the applied extensions another 12 weeks; at 5 hours/week, about 32 core weeks plus 7 extension weeks. Add a 20–30 hour integration project (roughly 4–10 weeks depending on pace) and buffer for difficult topics. A realistic total is approximately 42–74 weeks, depending on weekly availability and remediation.

Do not compress the work to meet an arbitrary calendar. The plan is designed around durable mastery, not completion speed.

## Suggested weekly allocation

| Activity | 3-hour week | 5-hour week |
|---|---:|---:|
| Core textbook / lecture | 1.0 h | 1.5 h |
| Primary source or research paper | 0.5 h | 1.0 h |
| Problem set / derivation | 1.0 h | 1.5 h |
| Coding / lab | 0.25 h | 0.75 h |
| Research log / synthesis | 0.25 h | 0.25 h |

If a problem set or lab needs more time, use the next week's allocation. Do not skip the derivation or testing stage to preserve a schedule.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Y1-01 Power Systems and Electricity Infrastructure** — build the electrical engineering bridge, complete the core problem sets plus the radial-feeder, IEEE 13-node, and flexible-interconnection applied assignments, and run the three-bus, radial-feeder, OpenDSS, and dynamic export-envelope models.
2. **Y1-02 Energy Economics and Market Fundamentals** — complete welfare and externality analysis, merit-order dispatch with carbon pricing and flexible demand, storage arbitrage, and non-exporting BTM storage optimization.
3. **Y1-03 Electricity Markets, Utility Regulation, and PJM** — study market clearing, congestion, revenue requirements, the Pennsylvania DER/PJM casebook, DER orchestration/BTM storage, and the advanced scarcity-pricing, resource-adequacy, and reserve co-optimization assignment.
4. **Y1-04 Applied Quantitative Methods** — complete OLS and robust inference, then panel-data and causal-identification exercises.
5. **Year 1 integration project** — refine the Pennsylvania distribution DER research question, finish the primary-source review, conduct the data-feasibility gate, and produce a policy brief or reproducible technical report.

## Weekly operating routine

At the start of each session, choose one measurable target: a chapter section, a derivation, a set of problems, or a code/test milestone. End the session by recording actual hours, what was completed, unresolved questions, and the next action in progress/research-log.md.

## Completion gate for each course

- Complete all required problem sets and review solution notes only after attempting the problems independently.
- Run the linked computational lab and understand its assumptions.
- Produce the final course project and self-assessment.
- Record evidence links and actual hours in the assessment tracker.
- Score the final project against the internal 24-point rubric in ACADEMIC_STANDARDS.md.

## Avoiding false progress

A generated explanation is not evidence of mastery. Re-derive key results without notes, change assumptions and predict the effect before running code, and explain why the model may fail outside its stated scope. Mark work completed only when the evidence exists in the repository.
