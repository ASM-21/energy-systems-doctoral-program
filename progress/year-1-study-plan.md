# Year 1 Study Plan

## Workload and calendar

The four course guides estimate 140–182 hours in total. The current progress tracker uses a planning baseline of approximately **165 hours** across the required problem sets, computational work, source analysis, and course deliverables. The Year 1 integration project is planned at a further **20–30 hours**.

At 3–5 hours per week, plan for roughly **37–65 weeks**, including the integration project. This is a range, not a deadline. Prerequisite remediation, difficult derivations, and optional extensions may increase it. Do not compress the work by skipping assessments or solution review.

The estimates are planning aids. Record actual time in the tracker and adjust future estimates from experience.

## Weekly allocation

| Activity | 3-hour week | 5-hour week |
|---|---:|---:|
| Core textbook or lecture | 1.0 h | 1.5 h |
| Primary source or research paper | 0.5 h | 1.0 h |
| Problem set or derivation | 1.0 h | 1.5 h |
| Coding or computational lab | 0.25 h | 0.75 h |
| Research log and retrieval practice | 0.25 h | 0.25 h |

A difficult derivation or lab can span multiple weeks. Keep the weekly budget stable and extend the calendar rather than rushing.

## Recommended sequence

The [curriculum map](../curriculum/course-map.md) is the canonical dependency guide.

1. **Y1-01 Power Systems and Electricity Infrastructure.** Build electrical-system literacy, per-unit and network representation, power-flow reasoning, feeder constraints, and DER integration. Work through the five problem sets and linked power-system labs.
2. **Y1-02 Energy Economics and Market Fundamentals.** Develop supply, demand, welfare, dispatch, externality, storage, and investment reasoning. Complete the four problem sets and linked economic-dispatch/storage labs.
3. **Y1-04 Applied Quantitative Methods.** Start once the data and programming prerequisites are comfortable. Build reproducible data workflows, OLS interpretation, measurement audits, and careful distinctions between description and causal identification.
4. **Y1-03 Electricity Markets, Utility Regulation, and PJM.** Apply engineering and economics to institutions, wholesale markets, utility regulation, resource adequacy, DER participation, and Pennsylvania interconnection. Use primary sources and record exact document versions and effective dates.
5. **Year 1 integration project.** Select one focused question, complete a literature and institutional review, establish data/model feasibility, conduct an analysis, and produce a technical report or policy brief with a reproducibility package.

Limited overlap is appropriate. Do not run all four courses at full intensity simultaneously.

## Weekly operating routine

At the beginning of a session, choose one measurable target: a section of a text, a derivation, a problem set section, a source review, or a code/test milestone. End by recording actual hours, completed evidence, unresolved questions, and the next action in progress/research-log.md.

## Problem sets and solutions

Attempt every assignment independently before consulting its worked solution. After comparing, classify errors as conceptual, mathematical, unit-related, computational, measurement-related, or interpretive. Correct the work and retain the revised derivation or code. A read solution is not evidence of mastery.

## Computational work

For each lab, read its README, inspect the input schema, install the listed dependencies, run the example and tests, trace at least one output back to its equations and inputs, and change at least one scenario parameter. Explain what the model excludes. Synthetic teaching models must not be described as operational utility estimates.

## Completion gate for each course

- Demonstrate the stated learning outcomes.
- Attempt and correct the required problem sets.
- Complete the linked computational or empirical work.
- Produce the course's final written or technical deliverable.
- Complete a self-assessment using the internal 24-point rubric.
- Record actual hours and evidence links in the progress trackers.

## Year 1 integration project

The project should reuse the knowledge and evidence developed in the courses. A suitable direction is a public-data analysis of distribution DER interconnection, utility investment, or PJM market/infrastructure issues. Do not assume public aggregate data can identify application-level outcomes or causal effects. Complete a data-feasibility assessment before committing to an empirical design.

Minimum deliverables:

1. Focused research question and motivation.
2. Literature and institutional review with traceable sources.
3. Data dictionary or model specification, including exclusions and limitations.
4. Reproducible analysis or engineering model.
5. Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis.
6. 1,000–1,500 word policy or technical brief.
7. Reproducibility instructions and a self-assessment.

## Avoiding false progress

A generated explanation is not evidence of mastery. Re-derive important results without notes, predict the effect of a parameter change before running code, and explain where a model may fail. Mark work completed only when the corresponding evidence exists.
