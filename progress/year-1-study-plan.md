# Year 1 Study Plan

## Time budget

The four core courses contain approximately 155 planned hours before optional applied extensions. The radial-feeder problem set and lab add approximately 8 hours to Y1-01. The carbon-pricing/demand-response and Pennsylvania DER aggregation extensions add approximately 8 more hours across Y1-02 and Y1-03. At 3 hours/week, that is about 52 weeks; at 5 hours/week, about 31 weeks. Add a 20–30 hour integration project and some buffer for difficult topics. The realistic Year 1 range is approximately 35–62 weeks for the core sequence, plus roughly 3–4 weeks for the current applied extensions depending on actual availability and remediation needs.

Do not compress the work to meet an arbitrary calendar. The plan is designed around durable mastery, not completion speed.

## Suggested weekly allocation

| Activity | 3-hour week | 5-hour week |
|---|---:|---:|
| Core textbook / lecture | 1.0 h | 1.5 h |
| Primary source or research paper | 0.5 h | 1.0 h |
| Problem set / derivation | 1.0 h | 1.5 h |
| Coding / lab | 0.25 h | 0.75 h |
| Research log / synthesis | 0.25 h | 0.25 h |

If a problem set or lab needs more time, use the next week's allocation. Do not skip the derivation or testing stage to preserve a schedule.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Y1-01 Power Systems and Electricity Infrastructure** — build the electrical engineering bridge, complete the two core problem sets plus the radial-feeder applied set, and run the three-bus and radial-feeder labs.
2. **Y1-02 Energy Economics and Market Fundamentals** — complete welfare and externality analysis, merit-order dispatch with carbon pricing and flexible demand, and storage arbitrage.
3. **Y1-03 Electricity Markets, Utility Regulation, and PJM** — study market clearing, congestion, revenue requirements, and the Pennsylvania DER/PJM casebook.
4. **Y1-04 Applied Quantitative Methods** — complete OLS and robust inference, then panel-data and causal-identification exercises.
5. **Year 1 integration project** — refine the Pennsylvania distribution DER research question, finish the primary-source review, conduct the data-feasibility gate, and produce a policy brief or reproducible technical report.

## Weekly operating routine

At the start of each session, choose one measurable target: a chapter section, a derivation, a set of problems, or a code/test milestone. End the session by recording actual hours, what was completed, unresolved questions, and the next action in progress/research-log.md.

## Completion gate for each course

- Complete both problem sets and review solution notes only after attempting the problems independently.
- Run the linked computational lab and understand its assumptions.
- Produce the final course project and self-assessment.
- Record evidence links and actual hours in the assessment tracker.
- Score the final project against the internal 24-point rubric in ACADEMIC_STANDARDS.md.

## Avoiding false progress

A generated explanation is not evidence of mastery. Re-derive key results without notes, change assumptions and predict the effect before running code, and explain why the model may fail outside its stated scope. Mark work completed only when the evidence exists in the repository.
