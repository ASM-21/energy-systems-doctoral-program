# Program Competency Framework and Evidence Crosswalk

This framework connects the six departments to observable learning outcomes, curriculum locations, and evidence. It is a curriculum-design and self-audit instrument, not an accreditation framework or external credential.

## How to read the framework

Competency codes are stable identifiers for course design and annual review. A course may contribute to a competency without establishing mastery. The learner should progress through four levels:

| Level | Meaning | Acceptable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Explain | Define concepts, identify institutions, state assumptions, and explain mechanisms | Closed-book explanation, annotated diagram, source note |
| 2 — Apply | Solve a structured problem or execute a documented method | Correct derivation, problem set, tested computational lab |
| 3 — Analyze | Select methods, compare alternatives, diagnose limitations, and defend interpretation | Technical memo, sensitivity analysis, empirical or engineering report |
| 4 — Research | Formulate an original question, justify a design, validate evidence, and communicate a defensible contribution | Reproducible research paper, model, presentation, and independent limitations review |

The levels are cumulative. A Level 4 claim requires evidence of the underlying Level 1–3 skills. The labels describe the expected cognitive depth, not a time-based promotion.

## Program-level learning outcomes

On completion of the full planned program, the learner should be able to:

1. **PLO-1 — Model physical infrastructure:** formulate and critically assess power-system representations, operational constraints, reliability, and DER integration.
2. **PLO-2 — Analyze economic mechanisms:** explain price formation, dispatch, market incentives, investment behavior, welfare, and cost allocation.
3. **PLO-3 — Interpret institutions and regulation:** trace jurisdiction, identify controlling primary authority, and evaluate regulatory incentives and implementation.
4. **PLO-4 — Use advanced quantitative methods:** derive and apply mathematical, statistical, econometric, optimization, and computational methods with appropriate diagnostics.
5. **PLO-5 — Evaluate transition and environmental outcomes:** quantify or critically assess emissions, lifecycle effects, reliability, affordability, and distributional trade-offs.
6. **PLO-6 — Assess strategic infrastructure decisions:** evaluate investment, resilience, energy security, supply chains, and industrial competitiveness using explicit outcomes and counterfactuals.
7. **PLO-7 — Conduct reproducible research:** create traceable datasets, validated models, transparent code, documented assumptions, and decision-useful communication.
8. **PLO-8 — Integrate disciplines:** connect engineering feasibility, economic incentives, institutional rules, and public outcomes without collapsing them into a single metric.

## Coverage gaps and development priorities

The current repository is strongest in introductory power-system models, energy economics, Pennsylvania DER institutions, reproducible data workflows, and core causal-inference methods. It is not yet a complete doctoral curriculum in every department. The following gaps should remain visible rather than being masked by the breadth of the project list.

| Priority | Gap | Why it matters | Planned treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AC power flow, unbalanced distribution, and validation beyond simplified screens | Simplified DC and first-order models omit voltage/reactive behavior, losses, and phase imbalance | Complete a matched reduced-network comparison between the radial approximation and an AC-capable solution; the current IEEE 13-node benchmark is not a matched validation pair |
| 1 | Utility finance, revenue requirements, and rate design | Investment incentives and customer cost allocation cannot be evaluated from dispatch economics alone | Y2-03 PS1–4 with worked solutions now cover revenue requirements, cost allocation, tariff design, regulatory incentives, and capital budgeting; the Pennsylvania applied case and computational synthesis remain before course activation |
| 1 | Market design, unit commitment, OPF, reserves, and market power | Current labs cover selected dispatch/congestion mechanics, not full market clearing or strategic behavior | Y2-04 course specification exists; develop and validate problem sets, unit-commitment and OPF labs, and a version-specific PJM case before activation |
| 2 | Advanced panel/event-study estimators and spatial/network interference | DER and infrastructure policies have spillovers, staggered implementation, and nonrandom exposure | Extend graduate econometrics with diagnostics and design comparisons |
| 2 | Climate policy evaluation and lifecycle system boundaries | Carbon and LCA questions require counterfactuals, marginal impacts, and distributional analysis | Develop a climate-economics module with empirical and scenario assignments |
| 2 | Project finance, cost of capital, and public investment appraisal | Infrastructure projects require financing, risk allocation, and social-versus-private return analysis | Add capital budgeting and regulatory finance problem sets |
| 3 | Energy security, supply-chain economics, and industrial policy | Strategic claims need measurable exposure, substitution, resilience, and cost-benefit frameworks | Build a comparative casebook with transparent indicators |

Priority 1 gaps should be developed before claiming broad specialization readiness. Priority 2 and 3 topics should follow the Year 1 foundation and relevant Year 2 methods. A research project may specialize before every gap is filled, but it must not imply comprehensive competence in unstudied areas.

## Annual curriculum quality review

At each annual review, evaluate the curriculum and learner evidence separately.

### Curriculum review

- Confirm that each active course has prerequisites, measurable outcomes, realistic workload, required/optional labels, assessment instructions, solution links, and a final deliverable.
- Check every course-to-competency link against actual assessment content. Remove a claimed outcome if no assignment tests it.
- Inspect worked solutions for mathematical correctness, units, assumptions, reproducibility, and explanatory value.
- Verify current regulatory, market, and data sources. Preserve publication, effective, access, and supersession dates.
- Check that synthetic demonstrations are visibly separated from empirical findings.
- Identify duplicate assignments and missing progression links.
- Update the coverage-gap register only when substantive materials and assessments exist.

### Learner evidence review

- Record only work actually completed; do not infer completion from repository content or passing automated tests.
- Select at least one artifact for each competency claimed at Level 3 or 4.
- Re-run computational artifacts from a clean environment.
- Reassess weak areas after a delay rather than relying on immediate solution recall.
- Record unresolved limitations and the next corrective action.

### Review record

Use the [annual review template](../progress/annual-review-template.md). For every proposed curriculum change, record the issue, evidence, decision, affected files, validation performed, and remaining limitations in the repository changelog or review notes.
