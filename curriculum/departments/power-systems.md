# Department: Power Systems Engineering

## Scope
Physical structure, operation, planning, and reliability of electric power systems from generation through transmission and distribution, including inverter-based resources, storage, DERs, and load flexibility.

## Competencies
- Three-phase fundamentals, phasors, complex power, and per-unit representation.
- Network models, admittance matrices, power flow, voltage and thermal constraints.
- Transmission and distribution topology; balanced and unbalanced distribution analysis.
- Faults, protection, stability, and reliability concepts.
- Economic dispatch, unit commitment, optimal power flow, and security constraints.
- DER interconnection, hosting capacity, inverter controls, storage, and flexible loads.
- Planning under uncertainty and changing load/resource mixes.

## References and tools
Grainger & Stevenson, *Power System Analysis*; Wood, Wollenberg & Sheblé, *Power Generation, Operation, and Control*; Kersting, *Distribution System Modeling and Analysis*; NERC reliability reports. Tools: Python, NumPy, SciPy, pandapower, PyPSA, OpenDSS, GridLAB-D, and MATLAB where licensed.

## Research questions
How do interconnection queues and distribution constraints affect DER deployment? How should distribution planning account for electrification and data-center loads? What are the reliability implications of changing resource mixes? How do hosting-capacity methods and flexibility affect upgrade costs?

## Evidence of mastery
A validated power-flow or distribution model, optimization exercise, technical report explaining assumptions and limitations, and translation of physical constraints into economic/regulatory implications.
