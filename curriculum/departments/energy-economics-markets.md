# Department: Energy Economics and Electricity Markets

## Scope
Economic behavior of energy producers and consumers, market design, price formation, investment, competition, and the interaction of wholesale markets with retail rates and utility planning.

## Competencies
Supply, demand, elasticity, welfare, externalities, and market failures; short-run marginal cost, merit order, dispatch, and price formation; LMP, congestion, losses, and scarcity pricing; day-ahead and real-time markets; ancillary services; capacity/resource adequacy; market power and market monitoring; utility cost recovery and rate design; economics of storage, DERs, demand response, and transmission.

## Analytical frameworks
Use partial equilibrium and welfare analysis for market questions; production-cost and optimization models for dispatch/planning; econometrics for empirical policy and market-performance questions. Be explicit about market boundaries, time resolution, and institutional rules.

## References
Stoft, *Power System Economics*; Kirschen & Strbac, *Fundamentals of Power System Economics*; Joskow's work on electricity restructuring and market design; FERC primers and RTO/ISO tariff/manual documents. Stanford ECON 253/256 and CMU 39-612/19-666 are catalog benchmarks.

## Research questions
How do capacity-market rules interact with energy-market revenues? Who bears transmission/distribution upgrade costs caused by new loads? How do retail rate structures affect DER adoption and peak demand? What is the economic value of flexibility under changing renewable penetration?
