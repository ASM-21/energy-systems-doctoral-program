# Department: Infrastructure Strategy and National Competitiveness

## Scope
Energy infrastructure as a strategic asset: capital formation, industrial competitiveness, supply chains, energy security, resilience, technology deployment, and public/private investment.

## Competencies
Infrastructure finance, capital budgeting, cost of capital, and project risk; energy security and critical infrastructure dependencies; industrial policy, domestic manufacturing, supply-chain concentration, and trade; large-load growth, data centers, electrification, and cost allocation; transmission/generation development timelines and institutional bottlenecks; scenario planning, strategic competition, and technology diffusion; public-private coordination and federal program design.

## Analytical discipline
Do not use "national security" or "competitiveness" as rhetorical shortcuts. Define the outcome, specify the counterfactual, identify who bears costs and receives benefits, and distinguish private profitability from social value.

## Research questions
How does electricity availability influence energy-intensive industry location? What are system costs and benefits of rapid data-center load growth? Which supply-chain vulnerabilities justify diversification or public intervention? How should resilience benefits enter regulated utility investment decisions?
