# Department: Quantitative and Computational Methods

## Scope
Mathematical modeling, statistical inference, econometrics, optimization, simulation, and reproducible computational research applied to energy systems and policy.

## Competencies
Linear algebra and matrix analysis; multivariable calculus, differential equations, and dynamical systems; probability and stochastic processes; convex analysis, constrained optimization, duality, and numerical optimization; graph theory and network optimization; statistical inference, regression, diagnostics, uncertainty quantification, and causal inference; panel data, time series, event studies, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables, and synthetic controls; LP, NLP, mixed-integer, stochastic, and robust optimization; power-system and production-cost modeling; data engineering, version control, testing, and reproducible research.

## Preferred tools
Python (pandas, NumPy, SciPy, statsmodels, Pyomo), Jupyter, Git, and domain tools (pandapower, PyPSA, OpenDSS). R is appropriate for econometrics. MATLAB may be used where it provides a meaningful advantage.

## Research standards
Every project includes environment specifications, data dictionary, source provenance, cleaning scripts, tests for key transformations, clear run order, and limitations. Avoid opaque notebooks that cannot be rerun from a clean environment.

## Research questions
Which empirical designs can identify interconnection-reform effects? How sensitive are resource-adequacy conclusions to correlated weather and outages? How can uncertainty be incorporated into distribution planning and DER valuation?

## Planned advanced mathematics sequence

The Year 2 mathematical methods course develops the analysis and numerical foundations needed for independent energy-system modeling. It includes proof techniques, matrix decompositions and conditioning, gradients and Hessians, stability of dynamical systems, stochastic processes, KKT conditions and duality, convergence of optimization algorithms, graph Laplacians, and stochastic/robust optimization. See [Advanced Mathematical Methods for Energy Systems and Infrastructure Research](../../courses/year-2/advanced-mathematics-energy-systems/README.md). This is a planned future course, not a Year 1 workload addition.
