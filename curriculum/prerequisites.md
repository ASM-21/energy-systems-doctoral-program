# Prerequisites and Diagnostic Bridge

The curriculum assumes graduate mechanical-engineering mathematics and scientific computing, but does not assume a completed electrical-engineering power sequence. A deliberate bridge is therefore required before advanced power-systems work.

## Entry assumptions

Expected existing preparation: calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, probability/statistics, engineering thermodynamics/energy systems, basic programming, and intermediate microeconomics or equivalent.

Do not infer competence from course titles alone. Use the diagnostic checks below.

## Power-systems bridge

Before the core distribution and power-flow sequence, be able to:
- manipulate complex numbers and phasors;
- analyze single-phase AC circuits and complex power;
- explain three-phase voltage/current relationships and line/phase quantities;
- calculate real/reactive/apparent power and power factor;
- work with transformer turns ratios and equivalent circuits;
- use per-unit normalization and convert between bases;
- interpret impedance and basic network topology.

**Diagnostic:** Solve a balanced three-phase load problem from line voltage, power, and power factor; calculate current and reactive power; convert a transformer/network impedance to per-unit on a stated base; explain the physical meaning of voltage drop and reactive power.

If any element is weak, use a focused AC-circuits/power-engineering review before beginning Y1-01. Do not spend months repeating broad undergraduate material unnecessarily.

## Mathematics and computation bridge

Confirm ability to:
- solve linear systems and interpret eigenvalues/eigenvectors;
- differentiate and optimize multivariable functions;
- formulate equality/inequality constrained optimization;
- work with probability distributions, expectation, variance, and sampling;
- implement numerical methods and diagnose convergence;
- use Python environments, functions, arrays, plotting, and Git.

**Diagnostic:** Formulate a least-cost dispatch LP, solve a small linear system, and write a Python script that reads a CSV, validates inputs, and produces a reproducible figure.

## Economics bridge

Confirm ability to:
- solve consumer and producer optimization problems;
- derive supply/demand equilibrium;
- calculate elasticities and welfare changes;
- distinguish marginal and average costs;
- explain externalities and natural monopoly;
- interpret a basic regression coefficient and confidence interval.

## Recommended gap-remediation approach

Use short, targeted remediation:
1. Identify the exact concept that fails.
2. Select one textbook chapter or high-quality lecture series.
3. Solve 5–10 problems without notes.
4. Record the derivation and any remaining uncertainty.
5. Return to the main course.

The goal is prerequisite fluency, not completion of another full degree.
